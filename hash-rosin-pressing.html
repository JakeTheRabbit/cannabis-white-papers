Hash rosin: heat, pressure and a micron screenHash rosin: heat, pressure and a micron screen

Rosin is a concentrate that you make with heat and force, without solvents. To make rosin, apply heat to the resin and push it through a mesh bag that has small holes. Then collect the oil that flows out. The steps before the press set the quality: the harvest, the wash and the drying. This paper shows all the steps from the trichome to the product.

Purpose and scope

A cannabis plant has a very large number of small glands. These glands are trichomes. Each trichome has a head on a stalk. On good flower, you can see the trichomes as white ‘crystals’.

Each trichome head is a very small bag of resin. Resin is a tacky oil that contains almost all of the potency, the aroma and the flavor of the plant. You make each solventless concentrate from these heads. You collect the heads and then press them with a small force. The method does not use chemical solvents. Examples of solvents are butane and alcohol.

The method has two parts:

  1. Make the hash. Remove the trichome heads from the plant and collect them as a powder or a paste. There are two usual methods. In the first method, you mix plant material in ice water. The heads break from the plant and fall in the water (bubble hash). In the second method, you rub dried material on small screens (dry sift).
  2. Press the hash into rosin. Seal the hash in a small mesh bag. Put the bag between two warm metal plates and apply force. The heat makes the resin soft. The pressure pushes the resin out through the mesh as an oil that has a gold color. This oil is rosin.

The method uses only heat plus pressure, and no solvents. Do not use too much heat or too much force. If the heat is too high, it removes the flavor. If the force is too high, it breaks the bag and pushes plant material through the mesh.

After the press, you do a cure of the rosin. In a cure, you keep the rosin at a set temperature, warm or cold. The cure sets the last texture of the rosin.

Then you can use the rosin as a dab, or put it in a vape cartridge. A dab is a small dose of rosin. You put the dose on a hot surface. The dose becomes vapor immediately, and you breathe the vapor.

TrichomeA small gland of resin on the plant. You can see it as a ‘crystal’. The head of the gland contains the oil that you want.
HashTrichome heads that you collect as a powder or a paste, before pressing.
RosinThe oil that you get from hash (or flower) when you use only heat and pressure.
SolventlessA product that you make with only force, heat, water and ice. You do not use butane, CO₂ or alcohol. Persons want these products because of this method.
Micron (µm)A micron is one thousandth of a millimeter. The micron of a press bag is the size of the holes in its mesh. A bag with a smaller micron has smaller holes. The rosin has less unwanted material, but the yield is lower.
CureA cure is a time in which you keep new rosin at a set temperature. The time is from hours to weeks. The cure sets the texture and the flavor of the rosin.
The primary information in this paper

The press cannot add quality. It can only decrease the quality of the material. A correct press run decreases it by a smaller quantity. The input material (the genetics, the harvest and the wash) sets the maximum quality. Heat, pressure, time and mesh size only change the part of this maximum that the rosin keeps.

In the next sections of this paper, the press has a set of controls: temperature, pressure, time and mesh size (‘micron’). The paper shows the effect of each control on the yield and the quality. It also shows the problems and how to correct them. If you do not know a term, go to the glossary at the end.

The primary information

Basic facts about the press

A rosin press has four controls: heat, pressure, time and screen (micron). All four controls change the balance of yield and quality (terpene content, color and clarity)[1]. Heat decreases the viscosity of the resin, thus the resin flows.

Cold resin has a high viscosity and does not flow easily. When the temperature increases a small quantity, the resin flows. The resin in a trichome has the same property. But the heat that lets the oil flow also removes the lightest aroma compounds.

The micron of the bag selects the materials that flow with the oil: only trichome oil, or also lipids and plant material. Pressure and time only help the oil to flow[2]. No control can increase the maximum quality that the steps before the press set.

The quality of the input sets the quality of the rosin

You cannot press 6★ rosin from 3★ input. The wash, the drying and the grade set the maximum quality. The press and the cure can only keep this maximum or decrease it. The press temperature for hash is lower than for flower. Clean full-melt hash has a blowout if you increase the pressure too quickly.

The method is short. Set the quality of the input and select the product. Select a micron that agrees with the trichome heads, and set the lowest temperature that lets the oil flow. Increase the pressure only to complete the flow. Collect the rosin cold and cure it to get the texture.

The steps

Rosin production steps

Hash rosin is the last part of a longer sequence of steps. The diagram shows the steps of the cold chain in blue. In these steps, heat decreases the quality. In the press and the cure, heat is a tool.

  1. 1
    Harvest
    Cut the plant at lights-off, when 80 to 90% of the trichomes are cloudy.
  2. 2
    Freeze or dry
    Freeze the plant immediately for live products. Dry the plant slowly for dry sift.
  3. 3
    Wash or dry sift
    Make bubble hash in ice water, or use dry sift screens. Do this work in a cold room and touch the material carefully.
  4. 4
    Dry the hash
    Use a freeze dryer. You can also dry the hash in cold air. This method has a low cost.
  5. 5
    Grade
    Melt a sample of the hash on a hot nail and give a grade from 1★ to 6★. The grade sets which products are possible.
  6. 6
    Conditioning
    Do a check of the moisture and make the hash cold. Make a pre-press puck for flower and dry sift only.
  7. 7
    Press
    Apply heat with no pressure. Increase the force slowly. Hold the force until the flow stops.
  8. 8
    Collect
    Use a cold tool. Collect the rosin on new parchment and put it immediately into cold glass.
  9. 9
    Select the product pathway
    The alternatives are badder, sauce, diamonds, cartridge oil and the core of a hash hole.
  10. 10
    Cure
    Do a cold cure, a warm cure or a crystallization in stages.
  11. 11
    Storage
    Keep the rosin in sealed glass, cold, with no light.

The next sections show each step with its values. The matrices and the problems show the control that you must change when a step has a problem.

Temperature ranges

Cold cure (after the press, not on the plates): 10 to 21 °C (50 to 70 °F) for days to approximately 2 weeks. This temperature makes a soft, smooth badder and keeps the monoterpenes[8].

Low-temperature press (for flavor): 60 to 85 °C (140 to 185 °F). This range gives the maximum terpene content and the highest lightness of the color. The range for hash is approximately 60 to 77 °C (140 to 171 °F). The range for flower of high quality is approximately 82 to 88 °C (180 to 190 °F).

Middle range (flower): 88 to 99 °C (190 to 210 °F). This range gives a high yield, and the terpenes decrease by a minimum quantity. It is a good start value if you do not know the flower.

High-temperature press (for yield, sauce and vape cartridges): 99 to 104 °C (210 to 219 °F). This range gives the maximum yield and the fastest flow, but the color is dark. Use it for material that has degradation[7].

The limits first

Data, conditions and unknown values

Known facts

  • The terpenes and the lightness of the color stay when the plate temperature is lower. The yield and the flow are higher when the temperature is higher, but the terpenes and the lightness decrease[1].
  • A bag with a smaller micron gives rosin with less unwanted material and a higher lightness, but the yield is lower. A bag with a larger micron has the opposite effect.
  • The temperature for hash and dry sift is lower than for flower, because they contain heads without plant material.
  • Heat in a sealed container keeps more terpenes than heat in open air. In a sealed container, the air above the rosin saturates with terpenes, thus the net evaporation stops. In open air, the oxygen causes oxidation of the terpenes and the air removes them[13]. The lightest monoterpenes have the highest rate of evaporation. They go out of the rosin first[16].
  • Only THCa can crystallize into diamonds. After decarboxylation to THC, it stays liquid[5]. For vape cartridges, full decarboxylation is necessary. For diamonds, the THCa must not change.

Conditions for the values in this paper

  • You make the hash correctly and you dry it correctly (with a freeze dryer or with cold air) before you put it between the plates.
  • Your press keeps the temperature accurately (PID control or control from an app) and applies the same heat on all the area of the two plates.
  • Your work area is clean and cold, and you record your runs. Thus you can change one control at a time.

Unknown properties that change the correct values

  • Size and range of size of the trichome heads. They are different for each cultivar. They have more effect on the micron than all other properties.
  • Quantity of lipids and waxes. It is different for each type of genetics and material. Flower contains more than hash.
  • Crystallization. The ratio of terpenes to THCa sets if diamonds crystallize easily or not easily.
  • Accurate moisture. A change of a small number of percent in RH changes the flow, the clarity and the risk of a blowout.
Basic model

Four connected press balances

As in a grow room, the press has a small number of connected balances. When you change one control, the other controls change.

B-01 · Heat
The two sides of this balance are the plate heat and the thermal mass of the bag. Sufficient heat melts the resin and decreases its viscosity, thus the resin flows. Too much heat removes terpenes and gives the rosin a dark color. Flow ↔ Flavor.
B-02 · Pressure and flow
The two sides of this balance are the applied force, and the resistance of the bag with the flow rate of the resin. Increase the force only after the oil starts to flow. If you increase the force slowly, the oil drains and stays clean. If you increase the force suddenly, the surface breaks and the oil sprays out. Too much force breaks the bag and pushes contaminants through the screen. Force ↔ Blowout.
B-03 · Moisture
The two sides of this balance are the water in the input, and the flow and the stable condition of the rosin. A small quantity of water helps the flow. Too much water causes an emulsion and haze, makes the rosin not stable, and increases the risk of a blowout. Correctly dried hash is the baseline. Flow ↔ Haze.
B-04 · Purity (screen)
The two sides of this balance are the oil that flows through the screen, and the contaminants that the screen stops. The micron sets the materials that flow through. A smaller micron stops more material (less unwanted material, lower yield). A larger micron lets more material flow through. Yield ↔ Clarity.
The primary balance

All four balances change the same pair of values: yield ↔ quality. Almost all adjustments increase one value and decrease the other. Before you change a control, make a decision about the value that is more important.

One change and its effects

Example: one change of the plate temperature

One small change causes other changes. Use full-melt fresh-frozen hash and increase the plate temperature from 71 °C → 77 °C (160 °F → 171 °F). Keep all other conditions the same.

Control that you change

Plate temperature +6 °C (+11 °F). The temperature is in the range for hash (60 to 77 °C / 140 to 171 °F). The bag is 36 µm. The ramp is slow.

First effects

  • The viscosity of the resin decreases. The seam of the bag becomes wet after a shorter time, and the oil flows more quickly.
  • The yield increases. Less oil stays in the puck.
  • The risk of a blowout decreases by a small quantity. The oil has a low viscosity and flows out before the pressure becomes high.

Secondary effects

Property that changesDirectionCause
Terpene content↓The monoterpenes with a high rate of evaporation (α-pinene, myrcene) start to go out of the rosin when the temperature increases[16]
Color↓ (dark color)More heat gives a stronger amber color

When to use the +6 °C (+11 °F) change

Use the change for hash with a low grade, or for cured hash, when the hash does not release oil. Also use the change when you make sauce, diamonds or material for vape cartridges. For these products, it is satisfactory when the terpenes decrease by a small quantity, for more flow and yield.

When not to use it

The change is not correct for 5★ to 6★ fresh-frozen hash that you press for live badder. A live badder has a strong terpene aroma. For this product, the six degrees decrease the aroma and increase the yield by only a small number of percent. This result is not good. The correct plate temperature is 71 °C (160 °F), and the yield is lower.

Step by step

Pressing methods for each step

This section shows all the steps from the plant to the dab, one step at a time, with the important values for each step. All the steps before the press are steps of the cold chain. In these steps, heat decreases the quality.

Harvest and trichome maturity

The harvest sets the maximum quality for all the next steps. The usual target is 80 to 90% cloudy capitate-stalked trichomes with 10 to 20% amber and no clear trichomes. This range is only a target. You can use other values. Hash makers frequently use less amber, because amber heads give rosin with a dark color and a greasy texture.

Many growers harvest at lights-off, but no known data show that a harvest at lights-off is necessary. For dry sift, harvest when the maturity of the trichomes is a small quantity lower. Stalks with too much ripeness break easily into small pieces. These pieces are contaminants[10].

Fresh-frozen material, or dry and cure

  • Fresh-frozen material (for live rosin and ice-water hash): Freeze the material immediately after you cut it. Do not dry it. This keeps the ‘live’ monoterpene profile. The press temperature for this material is the lowest, and the rosin has the highest purity.
  • Slow dry (for dry sift): Hang each plant at 15 to 18 °C (59 to 64 °F) and 55 to 60% RH, in full darkness, with a light airflow. Keep the plant there for 10 to 14 days. The stems must break and not bend. The water activity must be 0.58 to 0.62 aw (the safe range is 0.55 to 0.65, and 0.55 is the limit of too dry). Then freeze the material after trimming for 4 to 6 h (24 h is best). The stalks then break easily[10].

Ice-water wash (cold chain)

For bubble hash, mix fresh-frozen material in water that is almost frozen. The trichome heads break from the plant and fall through a stack of micron bags[14].

Alternative: dry sift (cold chain)

Do not use water. Do the work in a cold room (less than 10 °C / 50 °F). Use a card to move the frozen material across a stack of screens, from large holes to small holes. Use a light force for 3 to 5 min for each pass[10].

  • Screen stack: a work screen of 140 µm (110 LPI), a screen of 107 µm, and the primary screen of 70 µm (200 LPI). The material that falls through the 70 µm screen is edible grade. Heads of 70 to 120 µm are the best.
  • Static glove method: Make a glove of black nitrile cold in a freezer and use it on a 70 µm (200 LPI) screen. Move the glove in smooth circular movements, a small distance above the screen. The static charge lifts clean heads and the contaminants stay on the screen. Remove the heads from the glove, make the glove cold again and do 3 to 5 passes. Do not push the glove on the screen[4].

Drying of the hash (cold chain)

  • Freeze dryer (lyophilizer), best method: Set the condenser to −40 to −50 °C (−40 to −58 °F). Set the vacuum to approximately 100 to 200 mTorr (a pressure of less than approximately 500 mTorr is sufficient). Dry for 24 to 48 h, until the hash is fully dry. This method removes water without heat or oxidation.
  • Cold air drying (low cost): Use a microplane to put small pieces of the patties on parchment or screens. Dry them in a cold room for 24 to 72 h. This method has a higher risk of oxidation and contamination. Examine the hash for mold.

Grading and moisture conditioning

Melt a sample of the hash on a hot nail and give a grade of 1★ to 6★ (6★ = 95%+ heads, full-melt). The grade sets the products that you can make. It also sets how small a micron and how low a temperature you can use. Make the hash and the bag cold before pressing. Cold material in the bag gives a smaller number of blowouts.

5★ near full-melt85–95%Rosin of very good quality. Vape cartridge grade, with correct pressing.
4★ half-melt70–85%Good rosin. For vape cartridges, it can be necessary to clean the hash more.

1–2★<50%Only for edibles. It has too many contaminants for rosin.
6★ hash and the press

Many hash makers use 3★ to 4★ as the usual grade for the press. They keep 5★ to 6★ full-melt hash for a dab without a press[1]. This hash melts clean without a press. To press full-melt hash into rosin is a selection of texture and type of product. The press does not increase the quality.

Pre-press and bag load

  • Put 2 to 7 g (0.07 to 0.25 oz) in the inner bag. Fold the open end two times. Do not fill the bag too much. Keep space for the flow.
  • For diamonds, mechanical separation, and each run with a high risk of a blowout, put the inner bag in an outer bag of 120 to 160 µm.
  • For flower and dry sift, make a puck with high density and no air (a pre-press puck). The puck stops channels. For full-melt hash, do not do a pre-press with heat. At most, make a puck with a light force and without heat, to compress the load. Material of high grade flows without a puck.
  • Put the bag in folded parchment. The open seam must point to you.

The press

  1. Close the plates on the bag, or almost on the bag. Apply heat for 30 to 60 s with no pressure.
  2. Increase the force slowly in 15 to 20 s. Examine the seam. The seam becomes wet and the oil flows.
  3. Hold the target force for 60 to 120 s (a maximum of 180 s), or until the flow stops. Press until the flow stops, and not to a number[2].
  4. Release the force. The yield from clean 5★ to 6★ input is usually 60 to 80%[3]. If the yield is less than 50% with good input, a step before the press is incorrect.

Collection (cold chain)

Collect the rosin immediately with a cold dab tool on new parchment. Then put the rosin in glass that is cold and seal the glass. A cold collection keeps the terpenes and sets a clean texture. Go immediately to your product pathway.

The primary information in the matrix

Small heads and small particles go through a screen with a large micron, or make channels in it. This condition is the usual cause of a blowout. Use a smaller micron. Use a slower ramp. Do not fill the bag too much.

The step that you cannot correct

Do not start a diamond press at a high temperature. At a high temperature, THCa dissolves in the terpenes and flows out with them. The diamonds go out with the sauce[11]. The same occurs with salt in hot water. When the water is hotter, more salt stays in solution. Less salt crystallizes when the water becomes cold.

A press in steps at a low temperature removes the terpenes slowly. Then the THCa has no solvent and stays in the bag. Decarboxylation is a change in one direction only[15]. THC after decarboxylation cannot crystallize[5].

Pressure increases in sealed vessels

Use jars and lids that have a specification for this task. Keep the jars away from your face. Open a jar only after it is cold. The pressure in a sealed jar increases during decarboxylation and the jam method, because the rosin releases CO₂. Standard lids of mason jars release the pressure automatically at approximately 5 psi[13].

The problems

Frequent problems

bag
F-01 · Blowout
The bag breaks, and contaminants flow into the rosin. Causes: the pressure increases too quickly, the bag is too full, the micron is too large for the heads, or there is no outer bag. Correction: use an outer bag of 120 to 160 µm and a smaller load. Use a slow ramp of 15 to 20 s and a micron for the size of the heads. Apply the heat first. Then apply the force.
flow
F-02 · Low yield, no flow
The oil stops, or it goes back into the puck. Causes: the temperature is too low, the material is too dry, the pressure is too low, or the micron is too small for the heads. Correction: Increase the temperature by 3 to 6 °C (5 to 11 °F). Adjust the moisture. Use more force and a slower ramp. Use the next larger micron.
moisture
F-03 · Haze, not stable, wet
The rosin has haze and divides into parts. Causes: too much moisture (hash that is not fully dry, or wet flower) causes an emulsion, or the flower has many lipids. Correction: dry the input and adjust its moisture. Dry the hash fully with a freeze dryer. Adjust the flower to approximately 60% RH. Use a lower temperature and a bag with a smaller micron.
heat
F-04 · Weak aroma, low terpenes
The aroma is weak and the flavor is weak. Causes: the plate is too hot, the dwell time is too long, the collection is hot, or the storage is incorrect. Correction: use the range for a cold press. Decrease the dwell time. Collect the rosin cold. Keep it sealed and cold, with no light.
color
F-05 · Dark or green rosin
The color changes in the incorrect direction. Causes: the temperature is too high, the pressure is too high, or the input has a low grade and contaminants. Correction: decrease the temperature, use a slower ramp, use a smaller micron, and start with material of 5★ to 6★. Most color problems are problems of the input.
texture
F-06 · Badder without control, or no badder
The texture changes without your control. Causes: moisture in the input makes the rosin become badder with a greasy texture. Sap that is new has no nucleation. Correction: control the moisture of the input. To make badder with control, do a cold cure in a sealed jar at 13 to 16 °C (55 to 61 °F) for 24 to 72 h.
crystals
F-07 · Diamonds do not crystallize
There are no crystals and no terpene layer. Causes: the cure temperature is too high (the THCa stays in solution), the material has THC from decarboxylation, the rosin has no nucleation, or the terpene ratio is incorrect. Correction: decrease the temperature to approximately 38 °C (100 °F). Start with new rosin that has no decarboxylation and has nucleation. Press in steps. Do not start at a high temperature.
procedure
F-08 · Different results in each batch
Each run gives a different result. Causes: you do not control the moisture, you do not measure the ramp, or you have no record of the run. Correction: record each run (material, grade, micron, temperature, time, yield and result) and change one control at a time.
In sequence

Sequence of the press controls

Set the controls in this sequence. Each step sets a limit for the next step. If you do not do the first steps, the next steps cannot give a good result.

  1. 1
    Set the maximum quality of the input first
    The genetics, the harvest maturity, the quality of the wash or dry sift, and the moisture set the maximum. You cannot get good rosin from input of low quality with a press.
  2. 2
    Select the product
    The alternatives are badder, live rosin, diamonds, a vape cartridge or a hash hole. This selection sets the temperature range and the cure.
  3. 3
    Select the micron from the head size and the contaminants
    Large, clean heads: use a larger micron. Small heads or small particles: use a smaller micron. Do not select a small micron only because it is small.
  4. 4
    Set the temperature range for the material and the product
    Select the lowest temperature that lets the oil flow. The temperature for hash is low. The temperature for flower is higher. For material with degradation, the temperature is higher than for flower.
  5. 5
    Increase the pressure only to get the flow
    Heat is first. Force is second. The pressure only completes a flow that the heat started.
  6. 6
    Collect the rosin cold, then do a cure to get the texture
    A cold collection keeps the terpenes. The cure (cold, warm or in stages) sets if the product is badder, sauce or diamonds.
  7. 7
    Storage and check of the results
    Keep the rosin cold, with no light, in a sealed container that does not let air in. Record the yield and the grade. In the next run, adjust one control.

The sequence

Control of the rosin procedure

The most important information is the sequence:

The pressing sequence

Trichome grade and moisture set the maximum quality. Then heat decreases the viscosity of the resin. Then pressure pushes the oil through the screen. The micron selects the materials that go through: only oil, or oil with lipids and plant material.

The yield and the purity change in opposite directions. Then the collection temperature sets the texture. Then the cure sets if the product is badder, sauce or diamonds. Cold, sealed storage with no light keeps the product.

Use heat for the flow, the screen for purity and the pressure only to complete the flow. Then do a cure to get the texture. The steps before the press set the quality. The press can only keep the quality or decrease it.
