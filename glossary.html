Each term in the papers has a definition here. The definitions are for new growers.

3
3D model
A 3D model is an image of the building on a screen. You can turn it, change its size and select parts of it. The software makes it from the floor plan, and it is not a photo or render that does not change.
precision
A
Acclimation
Acclimation is the change in which a plant adapts to a higher light intensity during some weeks. The plant makes more chloroplasts, thicker leaf cuticles and a higher density of enzymes. Thus it can use the higher light intensity safely.
reference
Acclimatization (hardening)
Acclimatization (hardening) is the task to make a plantlet from tissue culture stronger. You decrease the humidity and increase the light gradually. Thus the plantlet does not die in usual air.
tissue culture
Action limit
An action limit is a value that you set before. When a reading is on the incorrect side of this value, a specified procedure is mandatory, and you must write the corrective action. An alert limit is a weaker limit that gives only an indication.
operationsprecision
Action threshold
An action threshold is a specified quantity of pests that starts a specified control procedure. For example, the quantity can be one pest that you see, or pests in more than 3% of a room.
reference
Advisory / prescription
An advisory (prescription) is an output that gives the task, the setpoint, the time limit and the result that will occur. It tells you: 'do this, by this time, or this result will occur'. It is not only an alarm.
reference
Air embolism
An air embolism is an air bubble in the stem after you cut it. It stops the uptake of water. You can prevent it if you put the cuttings in the substrate quickly and keep the ends of the cuttings wet.
reference
Air-filled porosity
Air-filled porosity is the part of the pore space of a substrate that holds air immediately after the substrate drains. When the air-filled porosity increases, the water-holding capacity decreases.
reference
Airlock / gowning cascade
An airlock (gowning cascade) is a sealed room between two spaces. Its doors cannot open at the same time. Personnel put on more protective clothing in the room before they go into a space with a higher grade.
reference
ALCOA+
ALCOA+ is a list of properties for data integrity. Records must be Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original and Accurate, plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring and Available.
reference
Aliasing
Aliasing is an effect in data that occurs when the sampling is too slow. The slow sampling changes a fast cycle into a slow cycle that does not occur in the plant.
reference
Alkalinity
Alkalinity is the capacity of water to neutralize acid. The capacity comes mostly from bicarbonates, and you give the value as ppm of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). Alkalinity is not the same as a high pH reading.
reference
Annex 11 / PI 041
Annex 11 and PI 041 give the GMP requirements for computerized systems and data integrity (access control, audit trails, validation and backups).
reference
Apical dominance
Apical dominance is the usual condition in which one primary top of a plant becomes higher than the side branches. Topping and LST stop apical dominance. Thus the growth of the plant is equal in all the branches.
reference
AppDaemon
AppDaemon is the Python software environment that operates the primary decision loop of Crop Steering, which has approximately 5900 lines, on Home Assistant.
precision
AppDaemon engine
The AppDaemon engine is the automatic controller (file master_crop_steering_app.py). It reads the sensors and the settings, selects the phase of each row, and operates the hardware sequence.
precision
Aseptic technique
Aseptic technique is the method to keep bacteria, fungi and yeast out of your culture. It is the most important method in tissue culture.
tissue culture
ASTM D8197
The industry standard for dry cannabis flower gives the range of 0.55 to 0.65 aw.
reference
Auto-tick (check that the system completes)
An auto-tick is a check item that the system records as done automatically, when its telemetry shows that the condition is correct. Thus the person does only the items that a sensor cannot examine.
operationsprecision
Autoflower seed
An autoflower seed is a seed with ruderalis genetics. The plant starts flowering automatically after a small number of weeks, and the photoperiod does not change this.
reference
Auxin
An auxin is a type of plant hormone that increases the growth of roots. IBA is the best auxin for cannabis.
tissue culturepropagation
B
Background drip baseline
The background drip baseline is the stable drip of the AC and the dehumidifier that the system finds at night. Then the system can subtract it from the total drain readings.
precision
Baseline / trajectory
The baseline (trajectory) is the learned envelope for a specified cultivar, growth stage and photoperiod phase. The system calculates it from the data of your previous crops.
precision
Batch / lot
A batch (lot) is one specified production run with one ID. The ID stays with the material in each transfer.
reference
Batch / Lot Record
A batch (lot) record is the document that records all the materials, tasks, personnel, equipment, results and disposition for one batch (FRM-MF-004). REG-CU-001 holds these records together.
reference
Batch release
Batch release is the controlled decision that a completed batch is safe and agrees with the specification. Test results and records give the data for the decision. The batch can go to a customer only after the decision.
quality
Bicarbonate (HCO3-)
Bicarbonate is the primary compound in the water that causes alkalinity. It neutralizes the acid that you add, and thus the pH increases again.
reference
Bioaerosol
Bioaerosol is a group of particles in the air that contain organisms. Examples are small pieces of skin with bacteria, fungal spores, and drops of liquid from a person who speaks or from a toilet flush.
health
Biocontrol / beneficial
A biocontrol, or beneficial organism, is an organism that you release to decrease the quantity of a pest. It can be a predator mite, a parasitoid wasp, or a microbe such as a fungus or a bacterium.
reference
Biological control (biocontrol)
Biological control (biocontrol) is the procedure in which you release predators, which are insects or mites that eat pests. The predators do not cause damage to the plant. When no prey is available, the number of predators decreases.
reference
Bleaching
Bleaching is a change of color in which the tissue becomes white or almost white. It occurs usually at the tips in the top of the canopy. Stress from light and oxidation causes it, and it is the result of photoinhibition that you can see.
reference
Blocked-dripper guard
The blocked-dripper guard is a fail-safe. It stops the irrigation of a row for approximately 2 hours after too many emergency shots fail (default 4 in 30 min). Each row has an on/off switch for it.
precision
Boundary layer
The boundary layer is the thin layer of air that does not move, on each leaf surface. CO2 that goes in, and water vapor and heat that go out, must diffuse slowly across the layer. Thus the layer sets a limit on the rate of work of the leaf. Air that moves (approximately 0.3 to 1.0 m/s at the canopy) makes the layer thinner. In a dense canopy, a layer in air that does not move sets a limit of CO2 that no room sensor shows.
reference
Bti / Btk
Bti and Btk are types of the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis. Bti (israelensis) kills the larvae of fungus gnats, and Btk (kurstaki) kills caterpillars. The two types kill the pest only when the pest eats them.
reference
Bucking
Bucking is the procedure in which you cut the buds from the stem. You do it after trimming.
reference
Bud rot (botrytis)
Bud rot (botrytis) is a gray mold that causes rot in buds with a high density, when the humidity is high. It is a primary risk in the last stage of flowering. Because of this risk, growers decrease the humidity.
reference
Bud set
Bud set is the time, approximately in weeks 3 to 4, when the pistils make small buds with a clear shape that become larger. Before this time, the pistils are at different positions.
reference
Buffer (buffering)
Buffering is the resistance of a substrate to a change of pH. Soil has high buffering, and coco and hydroponics have almost no buffering. Thus you must control the pH of coco and hydroponics with more precision.
reference
Buffer solution
A buffer solution is a liquid with a pH that you know accurately, for example 4.0 or 7.0. You use it to calibrate a pH pen.
reference
Buffering
Buffering is the capacity of a substrate to absorb errors in the pH or in the strength of the nutrients. Thus the environment of the roots is more stable.
reference
Buffering capacity
Buffering capacity is the resistance of a substrate or of water to a change of pH. High buffering gives a small change of pH (soil). Low buffering gives a fast change of pH (coco, hydroponics).
reference
Build back
Build back is the procedure in which you add minerals again to RO water or rainwater that has almost no minerals. The procedure is necessary because the plant must continue to get calcium, magnesium and some buffer. You usually add a CalMag supplement and the primary nutrients.
reference
Bulk EC
Bulk EC is the electrical conductivity of the mixture of the wet media (solids, water and air). The probe measures it directly in the range of 0-20000 microS/cm.
reference
Bulking
Bulking is the middle phase of flowering (approximately weeks 5 to 7). In this phase, the mass of the buds increases at the highest rate.
reference
Burping
Burping is a short opening of a sealed container during curing. The opening lets moist air go out of the container and lets new air go into it.
reference
C
C1D1
C1D1 (Class 1 Division 1) is a classification for areas where flammable solvent vapor can be in the air. It is for electrical equipment and rooms. These areas must have explosion-proof equipment and gas detection.
reference
Cal-mag
Cal-mag is a supplement of calcium and magnesium. It is necessary when you use coco, to prevent the deficiency that the CEC of the coco causes.
reference
Calibration
Calibration is the adjustment of the reading of a sensor, to make it agree with the correct value for the substrate that you use. Without calibration, the percentage on the screen can be incorrect.
sensors
Calm dashboard
A calm dashboard is a screen that shows exceptions. Usually the screen gives no information. It tells you only when there is important information.
precision
CalMag
CalMag is a supplement of calcium and magnesium, frequently with iron. You add it to water that has a small quantity of minerals, such as RO water, rainwater or very soft tap water. It prevents a deficiency of calcium and magnesium.
reference
Calvin cycle
The Calvin cycle is the half of photosynthesis that uses CO2. It changes the light energy that the plant absorbs into sugar. If the concentration of CO2 is too low, this stage cannot operate at the same rate as the light reactions.
reference
Calyx
A calyx is one small part of the flower, in the shape of a drop. Many calyxes together make a bud.
reference
Cannabis ruderalis
Cannabis ruderalis is a strong subspecies of cannabis. Its genetics give autoflowers the automatic flowering that the photoperiod does not control.
reference
CAPA
CAPA (Corrective And Preventive Action) is the standard procedure to repair a problem and to make sure that it does not occur again. It is one of the primary procedures of GMP.
quality
CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action)
CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) is a procedure that starts from the root cause. Corrective action repairs a problem that occurred. Preventive action makes sure that a problem does not occur, or does not occur again.
reference
Capacitance sensor
A capacitance sensor is a probe that measures the quantity of water in the substrate. It senses the electrical 'permittivity' of the substrate. Water changes the permittivity much more than soil or air does.
sensors
Carbonate / bicarbonate
Carbonate and bicarbonate (CO3 and HCO3) are the compounds in solution that cause alkalinity. They neutralize the acid that you add. If the quantity is too large, they can cause lockout of calcium and magnesium.
reference
CCP (Critical Control Point)
A CCP (Critical Control Point) is a step in an operation, with a measured limit that prevents a specified hazard. It is a term from HACCP for control during the operation.
reference
CEC (cation exchange capacity)
CEC (cation exchange capacity) shows how strongly a substrate holds cations, which are nutrient ions with a positive electric charge (in meq/100g). A high CEC gives more buffering of errors in the feed.
reference
Change Control
The standard procedure for each change that can change the quality of the product or the validated state is change control. In this procedure, you examine the change, give approval for it and then make it.
reference
Channeling (preferential flow)
Channeling (preferential flow) is water that flows straight down through the substrate. It does not touch the roots, and frequently it does not touch the sensor.
precision
Chloramine
Chloramine is a disinfectant of chlorine and ammonia that is more stable than chlorine. It does not go out of the water as a gas when you keep the water in a container. To remove it, you must use a catalytic carbon filter or reverse osmosis.
reference
Chlorine
Chlorine is a disinfectant that suppliers frequently add to tap water. It moves from the water into the air as a gas in approximately 24 hours if you keep the water in a container with aeration. Chlorine can cause damage to the tips of the roots and to beneficial microbes at low concentrations.
reference
Chloroplast
A chloroplast is the structure in a cell that does photosynthesis. During acclimation, plants make more chloroplasts. Thus they can use a higher light intensity.
reference
Chlorosis
Chlorosis is the condition in which leaf tissue becomes yellow because the quantity of green chlorophyll decreases.
reference
Cleanroom grade
A cleanroom grade shows the maximum number of airborne particles that a room can have. To control the contamination of the product, you use a grade with a smaller maximum number of particles, and you use gowning.
qualityfacility
Cleanroom grade (CNC, D, C, B, A)
A cleanroom grade shows how clean the air of a room is. In CNC, the air has no control, and no other grade has air as clean as Grade A. The grades agree with the ISO 14644 classes (D=ISO 8, C=ISO 7, A=ISO 5).
reference
Clone
A clone is a copy of a plant that has the same genes as the plant. You can make a clone from a cutting or with tissue culture.
propagation
Closed loop
A closed loop is a control system in which the result goes back to cause the next step. The steps are: measure, select, adjust, and then measure the effect of the adjustment. The system does these steps again, continuously.
reference
Closed loop (with approval)
In a gated closed loop, the system does automatically only the tasks that have a low risk and a specified license. A person must give approval for each task that is permanent or has a high cost.
precision
Closed-loop control
Closed-loop control is a system that measures a result, compares the result with the value that you want, and makes adjustments automatically. A thermostat is a typical example.
control
CO2 supplementation
CO2 supplementation is a procedure in which you add carbon dioxide to the air. The concentration of carbon dioxide is then more than the usual value in the air (approximately 400 to 600 ppm). It agrees with a high light intensity, and it is necessary to prevent bleaching at intensities such as 1200 or 1500 µmol/m²/s.
reference
CoA (Certificate of Analysis)
A CoA (Certificate of Analysis) is the document that gives a list of the test results of a batch and compares them with the specification. You supply it at release.
reference
Coco coir
Coco coir is a substrate of coconut husk that you use as an alternative to soil. Water flows out of it quickly, and it changes quickly when you apply water.
precision
Coefficient of variation (CV)
The coefficient of variation (CV) shows how large the variation is, in relation to the average. For a batch of plants, the CV measures the noise (the differences between the plants) in your crop.
reference
Cola
A cola is a large group of buds with a high density on a primary stem. The cola is usually the flower at the top of the plant, and it has the best growth.
reference
Common-cause variation
Common-cause variation is the usual small random change of a stable operation. It stays in the control limits. You must not adjust the operation for this variation.
reference
Common-cause variation and special-cause variation
Common-cause variation is the usual small random change, and you do not adjust the operation for it. Special-cause variation is an unusual change, for example a point that is out of a limit or a sequence of points that is not random. An investigation is necessary for it.
reference
Confidence
Confidence is the value that the system gives to show how accurate its estimate is at this time. When the confidence is high, the system can apply water. When the confidence is low, the system must be careful.
precision
Control limits
Control limits are limits that you calculate from the data that you recorded for the operation (typically the mean ± 3 standard deviations). They divide the usual variation from the unusual variation.
reference
Control limits (SPC)
Control limits are limits around the usual variation, and you calculate them with statistics. A reading in the limits is only noise, and a reading out of the limits is a signal for a correction. SPC is the short name of 'statistical process control'.
control
Cotyledons
Cotyledons, or seed leaves, are the two rounded leaves of the embryo that you see first. They contain nutrients and supply them to the seedling.
reference
Coupled system
A coupled system is a system in which a change to one control changes some other controls at the same time. Thus no lever operates independently.
reference
Crop steering
Crop steering is a method to cause a plant to make more vegetative growth (leaves) or more generative growth (flowers and fruit). You control the irrigation, the climate and the light.
crop steering
Crop Steering AI (CS)
Crop Steering AI (CS) is an irrigation system with 4 phases. It uses sensor data, and it has control for each zone, EC logic and ML prediction. It is the irrigation controller that operates at this time.
precision
Cross-contamination
Cross-contamination is the movement of contamination from a dirty area or item to a clean area or item. For example, a person goes from the flower area back to the propagation area and does not put on a gown again.
operationshealth
Culling
Culling is the procedure in which you divide the clones into groups before transplant. You remove the clones that are weak or do not have roots. The groups are keepers, rejects and kills.
reference
Curing
Curing is the procedure in which a completed batch becomes stable, with the same water activity in all the flower. During curing, you keep the terpenes and prevent mold.
reference
Cutting
A cutting is a shoot that you cut from a plant. It makes roots and becomes a clone. The terms cutting and clone are interchangeable.
reference
Cytokinin
A cytokinin is a type of plant hormone that increases the growth of shoots and buds. The examples are meta-topolin, BAP and TDZ.
tissue culture
D
Damping-off
Damping-off is a fungal disease (Pythium, Fusarium, Rhizoctonia). It causes rot in the seedling at the level of the soil, and it usually kills the seedling.
reference
Dead-band
A dead-band is the tolerance range in which a controller does not make a correction. If the dead-band is too small, the equipment makes too many adjustments (hunting).
reference
Defanning / Defoliation
Defanning (defoliation) is the removal of selected large fan leaves. It opens the canopy. Thus light goes into the canopy and air flows through it.
reference
Deficiency
A deficiency is the condition in which the plant does not get a sufficient quantity of a nutrient. The plant has less color, becomes yellow, and its growth is slow.
reference
Defoliation
Defoliation is the removal of selected leaves. Thus more light and more air go into the canopy.
reference
Dehumidification load
The dehumidification load is the quantity of water that a room must remove from the air each day to keep its humidity target. The transpiration of the canopy sets the load, and at a high light intensity the load is frequently the limit of the yield. The unit is liters or US pints each day (1 liter is approximately 2.1 pints), and a dense flowering canopy can transpire some liters for each square meter each day.
reference
Deviation
A deviation is each difference from the approved procedure in the documents. You must record it and do an investigation.
reference
Digital twin
A digital twin is a 3D model connected to the data of sensors in the facility. Thus it shows the temperature, the humidity and the equipment status at this time.
precision
Dilution
Dilution is the procedure in which you mix a material equally in a large volume of water. Thus the concentration is low. This low concentration makes it safe to add Part B after Part A.
reference
Dissolved oxygen (DO)
Dissolved oxygen (DO) is the quantity of oxygen in solution in water, and the roots must have it. DO decreases when the temperature of the water increases, from approximately 5 to 6 ppm at 20 °C to 3 to 4 ppm at 26 °C. A low DO increases the risk of root disease.
reference
DLI
DLI (daily light integral) is the total quantity of light that a plant receives and can use in one day, in mol/m²/day. You calculate DLI from the light intensity (PPFD) and the hours of light. Thus two different intensities can give the same DLI with different photoperiods.
reference
DLI (Daily Light Integral)
DLI (daily light integral) is the total number of PAR photons that a surface receives in one day, in moles for each square meter (mol/m2/day). DLI = PPFD x seconds of light in each day / 1,000,000. It is the dose that has the largest effect on the yield.
reference
Dome
A dome is a transparent plastic lid for humidity. You put it on a clone tray to keep moisture around the cuttings that do not have roots.
reference
Draw call
A draw call is one instruction to the graphics card to show an object. When the number of draw calls is smaller, the model is faster (keep the number less than approximately 300).
precision
Drench
A drench is a treatment that you apply to the substrate and the root zone, and not to the foliage.
reference
Drift
Drift is a slow sensor error that is always in the same direction, and it shows a trend that does not occur in the plant. It is the dangerous type of noise. Only calibration and a check with other data can remove it.
reference
Dry-to-wet ratio
The dry-to-wet ratio is the last dry weight as a fraction of the initial wet weight. It is approximately 10% in the source data.
reference
Dryback
Dryback is the quantity by which the moisture in the root zone decreases between irrigations. A grower sets the dryback to cause generative growth or vegetative growth in the plant.
crop steering
Dryback %
Dryback % is the size of a dryback, in percentage points of water content. It is the peak of each day minus the next trough. For example, 78% to 58% is a dryback of 20 points.
crop steering
DUL (drained upper limit) / container capacity
DUL (drained upper limit), or container capacity, is the quantity of water that a container holds after free drainage stops. It is a value that you find for each pot, and it is not a constant value for the substrate. It changes slowly when the roots become larger.
reference
DWC (deep water culture)
DWC (deep water culture) is hydroponics in which the roots hang in nutrient water with oxygen, and there is no solid medium. An air pump supplies the oxygen.
reference
E
EC
EC is electrical conductivity. It shows the strength of the nutrients in the feed water, in mS/cm.
reference
EC (electrical conductivity)
EC gives an estimate of the quantity of fertilizer salt that is in solution in the root zone. When the EC is higher, the feed is stronger.
crop steeringsensors
EC ratio
The EC ratio is the measured EC divided by the target EC. When the ratio is more than 1, the salt strength is too high (apply more water to dilute). When the ratio is less than 1, the salt strength is too weak (apply less water).
precision
Efficacy (umol/J)
Efficacy is the number of PAR photons that a fixture makes for each joule of electricity (umol/J). It is the primary number for the efficiency when you compare lights. Good LEDs have approximately 2.7 to 3.0 umol/J, HPS lights have approximately 1.7 to 1.9, and CMH lights have approximately 1.3 to 1.9.
reference
EM (Environmental Monitoring)
EM (Environmental Monitoring) is the procedure to get samples of non-viable particles, viable air, surfaces and personnel. You compare the results with the alert limits and the action limits.
reference
Embryo
The embryo is the very small plant in the seed. It contains a root, two cotyledons and a shoot.
reference
Endophyte
An endophyte is a microbe that is in the tissue of a plant that looks in good condition. It is the contaminant that you cannot easily remove in tissue culture, because bleach on the surface cannot touch it.
tissue culturedisease
Entomopathogenic nematodes
Entomopathogenic nematodes are very small worms, for example Steinernema feltiae. You apply them as a drench to the substrate. They are parasites of fungus gnat larvae, and they kill the larvae.
reference
ESPHome
ESPHome is the firmware platform that operates the hardware sensors and some switches. It sends the data to Home Assistant.
precision
EWMA
EWMA is the exponentially-weighted moving average. It is a rolling average that gives more weight to the newest readings. Thus it has less lag for the same smoothing.
reference
Exclusion
Exclusion is the procedure in which you control the persons and the items that go into a room with pests. It stops pests that move from one room to a different room.
reference
Explant
An explant is a small piece of a plant. You cut it from the plant and put it in the culture vessel to start a new culture.
tissue culture
Extrude
To extrude is to extend a flat 2D shape (for example, the shape of a floor) up, to give it thickness or height.
precision
F
Fail-safe read
In a fail-safe read, the engine cannot find the condition of the primary arm switch. The engine then uses the condition OFF and does not apply a shot. Thus a condition that is not synchronized cannot cause the engine to operate without data.
precision
Fan leaves
Fan leaves are the large leaves that have a small number of trichomes. You remove them by hand at the start of trimming.
reference
Far-red
Far-red is light of approximately 700 to 750 nm, immediately after the PAR range. Phytochrome senses far-red light, and far-red light can make the plant sense the start of the night more quickly. Far-red light causes some stretch of the stem, and high doses of it can decrease the density of the buds.
reference
Feedback
In feedback, a system uses the measured result of an operation to select the next operation. Feedback is the primary part of each closed-loop system, which makes corrections automatically.
control
Feminized seed
A feminized seed is a seed that a breeder makes to give almost only female plants. Thus it is not necessary to identify and remove male plants.
reference
Field capacity
Field capacity is the maximum VWC that the medium can hold before water flows out. The default maximum is 70%.
precision
Field capacity / substrate EC
The substrate EC is the concentration of salt in the substrate of the root zone. The Athena guide gives targets for each stage, for example 3 to 5 in the vegetative stage. You use the mixed feed to increase the substrate EC to these targets.
reference
FIM
FIM is a topping in which you cut most of the top tip but not all of it. FIM can make some new tops. A topping that removes all the tip makes only two new tops.
reference
Fit-out
The fit-out is the equipment and furniture in the bare shell of the building: benches, lights, dehumidifiers, tanks and racks.
precision
Flip (12/12)
The flip is the change of the grow light timer to 12 hours on and 12 hours off. The change causes flowering in photoperiod plants.
reference
Floor plan
A floor plan is a flat drawing from an architect. It shows the walls, doors and rooms from above, usually with dimensions in millimeters.
precision
Florigen
Florigen is a protein that moves in the plant and is the flowering signal. The leaves make it when the nights are sufficiently long. It moves to the growth tips, and it causes them to make buds and not leaves.
reference
Flush
A flush is the procedure in which you apply water that flows through the substrate and removes the salts that collect. The water has the correct pH, and it has no fertilizer or a small quantity of fertilizer. Thus the root zone is in its initial condition again.
reference
Foliar spray
A foliar spray is a liquid treatment that you apply to the leaves and the stems, and not to the root zone.
reference
Fomite
A fomite is an object that holds microbes and moves them to a different object. Examples are a phone, a pen, a tool or a handle. Hands move contamination from fomites to the crop.
health
FOV cone
An FOV cone is a transparent shape that shows the area in the field of view of a camera. The floor out of all the cones is a possible blind spot.
precision
Frass
Frass is waste from insects and small pieces of material. During scouting, you can see frass as one of the first signs of pests.
reference
Full pot (DUL, drained upper limit)
The full pot (DUL, drained upper limit) is the maximum quantity of water that a pot can hold after drainage.
precision
G
GACP
GACP (Good Agricultural and Collection Practice) is the standard for cultivation and drying, before the GMP facility. It supplies biomass that agrees with GMP to the facility.
reference
GACP (Good Agricultural and Collection Practice)
GACP (Good Agricultural and Collection Practice) is the standard for the cultivation and collection of the starting material of medicinal cannabis. In this standard, the material is a plant crop before it becomes a medicine.
reference
GDP (Good Documentation Practice)
GDP (Good Documentation Practice) is a group of procedures to make and to correct records. You make a record at the time of the task, and you cannot erase it. A correction is one line through the error, with initials, a date and a cause, and the initial entry stays easy to read.
reference
Genealogy / traceability
Genealogy (traceability) is the record that connects each completed unit to its source lots, operators, equipment and conditions, forward and back. It makes possible a recall of specified lots only.
reference
Generalist and specialist predator
A generalist predator eats many types of pest and also eats pollen. It stays in the crop when there is no prey. A specialist predator eats one type of pest quickly, but its number decreases when there is no prey of that type.

Reverse osmosis (RO) is filtration in which you push water through a semipermeable membrane. The membrane removes most of the minerals in solution, and the water has almost no PPM (usually 0 to 10 ppm TDS).

RH
RH is relative humidity. It is the percentage of moisture that the air contains compared with the maximum quantity that the air can contain at that temperature.

Ripening
Ripening is the last phase before harvest. In this phase, the pistils darken, the trichomes come to maturity, and the plant moves the nutrients that it keeps into the buds.

Rockwool (stonewool)
Rockwool (stone wool) is an inert medium of fiber from molten rock. It drains freely, it holds a large quantity of water, and it has almost no buffering. It is alkaline until you soak it.

Rockwool cube
A rockwool cube is a plug of mineral fiber that holds water and air. A cutting makes roots in it. A peat plug has the same function.

Root zone
The root zone is the wet substrate directly around the roots. The uptake of nutrients and water occurs there.

Rooting hormone / IBA
A rooting hormone is a gel or liquid that contains indole-3-butyric acid (IBA), which is a synthetic auxin. Auxin is a plant hormone for roots. You apply the hormone to a stem after you cut the stem, to cause the stem to make roots.

Signal and noise
A signal is a change that occurs in the plant or in the root zone. Noise is a small random change from the sensor or from the environment, and it does not show a change in the plant. Good control uses the signal and ignores the noise.

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is the strength of the signal compared with the strength of all other changes in the data. When the SNR is high, the data are stable and easy to read. When the SNR is low, the data have many changes, and the control adjusts for each change.
