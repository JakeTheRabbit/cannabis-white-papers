Integrated pest management blueprint for indoor medicinal cannabis in AucklandIntegrated pest management blueprint for indoor medicinal cannabis in Auckland

This blueprint is a system to keep pests and diseases out of an indoor facility for medicinal cannabis. The system has clean starting material, sure identification, control methods that the New Zealand regulations accept, and monitoring during the crop cycle. It also has a written record that includes the first finding, the corrective action, and the batch hold or release.

01 · Read this first

Purpose and scope

This blueprint is for the operation of an indoor medicinal cannabis facility in Auckland. It connects the identification of pests and diseases to clean stock and to the safety of workers. It also connects the identification to the regulations for products and organisms in New Zealand. It connects the identification to the release of batches after the residue test, to traceability and to CAPA. We do not want you to have the most sprays. We want you to have an alternative when a pest or disease problem and a compliance problem occur at the same time.

We used the supplied 128-page IPM Book V15 to compare the coverage of this paper with the book[10]. The book includes these parts: the basic information about IPM, cultural controls, environmental controls, biological controls and chemical controls. It also includes how to make a program, eight arthropod profiles, six disease profiles, aids for identification, a glossary and tools for operators. This paper is not a copy of the book. It does not contain the commercial programs of the book, its figures or its rates. For this paper, sources of the authorities in New Zealand and primary literature are more important than the book.

An AI tool made the photos for training

Use a photo to select where to look and which part of the plant to collect as a sample. Do not use a photo to make sure that you know the species. An AI tool made each photo in this paper. Microscopy is necessary for broad mites and russet mites, and RT-qPCR or RT-PCR is necessary for HLVd. A diagnostic laboratory is frequently necessary for root diseases and leaf diseases. A photo that shows the correct signs is not a test result.

  • The current status is more important than this paper. Examine the current information each time that you get or use a product. Use the information from the Ministry, ACVM, EPA, WorkSafe, the label and the SDS.
  • The thresholds of a facility are controlled values. The numbers in this blueprint are examples or start values. Use a number from this blueprint only if your approved SOP includes it.
  • Previous damage stays on the plant. Examine living organisms, new lesions, new growth, traps, roots or laboratory results. Use them to make sure that a control had the correct effect. A plant that is in better condition is not a sign of the effect.
  • Clean stock is the most important part. A problem in the mother room increases in each clone lot. Thus give the mother room the best protection.
02 · Reference

Definitions

This section gives the terms for biology, diagnosis, operation and the regulations of New Zealand. The blueprint uses these terms.

Action thresholdAn action threshold is a value or a finding that you agree before the scouting. The threshold changes the task from monitoring to a response. It can be a number of organisms, a trend in some readings, a severity value, or one confirmed finding when the tolerance is zero.
AlateAn aphid with wings. A colony makes aphids with wings when it has too many aphids. These aphids can go away and start a new colony.
AntagonistA microbe that is a beneficial organism and that controls a plant pathogen. It uses the same space and nutrients as the pathogen, it makes substances that stop the pathogen, or it controls the pathogen by other methods.
AsexualThe production of offspring without sex. Indoors, most groups of pests and pathogens increase in number by this method.
AxilThe area where a leaf or branch connects to the stem. This area has protection, and thus it is important to examine it.
BiosecurityThe controls that stop the entry of pests and pathogens into the facility, and that stop their movement in the facility.
BronzingA change of color to bronze-brown. Injury from pests that eat the plant, or stress, causes it. The term shows the color that you see. It does not give the name of the cause.
CAPACorrective and preventive action. Put the event in containment, find the cause, and correct it. Change the condition that let the event occur. Then make sure that the change had an effect.
ChlorosisThe green color of the plant tissue becomes less.
Conidium / conidiaA spore of a fungus that the fungus makes without sex. It is for the movement of the fungus to other plants. One spore is a conidium. More than one spore are conidia.
CornicleOne of the two short tubes near the rear of an aphid. The cornicles are the sign that most quickly confirms that an insect is an aphid.
Crown rotDisease at the crown, where the stem connects to the root system.
Cupping / tacoingLeaf edges that bend up or down. Heat, humidity, chemical injury and mite damage cause it. Thus examine the plant for the organism before you make a decision on the cause.
Damping-offA seedling or cutting collapses suddenly because of root pathogens or crown pathogens. Pythium or Fusarium frequently cause it.
Dew-point marginThe dew-point margin is the difference between the temperature of the leaf or surface and the dew point of the air around it. When the temperature of the surface is less than the dew point, condensation occurs. If the dew-point margin is small, condensation is near.
Dissolved oxygenThe oxygen in the irrigation solution. Roots must have it. When the quantity is low, the roots have stress, and the conditions help some root diseases.
DuddingA term of production for weak cannabis plants with slow growth and flowers with weak development. The term shows how the plants look. It does not give a diagnosis, and it is not a different name for HLVd.
EntomopathogenicAn organism that can infect and kill insects or mites. The term is for the fungi, bacteria and nematodes that you use as biological controls.
ExclusionAll the controls that stop the entry of a pest, a pathogen or material that has no approval into the controlled crop system.
FRAC / IRACWhen you use one mode of action again and again, the pests or pathogens that stay are the most resistant to the product. These organisms increase in number. The name of this effect is resistance. FRAC and IRAC are the systems that put fungicides and insecticides in groups by mode of action. Thus a program can change from one group to a different group, and does not apply the same pressure again and again.
HoneydewA tacky liquid with sugar. Aphids, whiteflies and other insects that eat sap make it as waste.
IncidenceIncidence is the proportion of plants or sites that have the problem. You record incidence and severity as two different values.
InoculumPathogen material that can start a new infection, for example spores, debris with the infection, or water with contamination.
InstarThe stage of an arthropod that is not an adult, between one molt and the next molt.
LarvaA stage that is not an adult and that eats. It does not look the same as the adult. In fungus gnats, thrips and caterpillars, the larva causes the damage and it is important to identify it.
LOQLimit of quantification. The lowest concentration for which a laboratory method gives a result with satisfactory performance. At a concentration less than the LOQ, the method cannot give a number.
MeristemThe tissue at a shoot tip that divides and makes all the new growth above it.
MyceliumThe group of thin tubes that is the body of a fungus.
MoldGrowth of a fungus that you can see. The standard spelling in New Zealand is different from the spelling in this paper. Imported references use the spelling of this paper. The two spellings are for the same growth.
NecrosisPlant tissue that died.
NymphA stage of an arthropod that looks the same as the adult, but is not an adult.
OomyceteAn organism with some of the properties of a fungus, from a group that is different from the fungi. Pythium is an oomycete. It is not a fungus.
OosporeA spore with thick walls for survival, which some oomycetes make. It can stay in a system between crops.
ParasiteAn organism that is in or on a host and gets nutrients from the host. Usually the parasite does not kill the host. A parasitoid kills the one host that it uses.
ParasitoidAn organism, in IPM usually a small wasp. The stage of the parasitoid that is not an adult is in or on one host and kills the host.
ParthenogenesisParthenogenesis is the production of offspring without fertilization. Thus one female can start a colony. The number of pests increases without the time that is necessary to find a male. The number of some indoor pests increases by this method.
PathogenAn organism or agent that causes disease.
PetioleThe stalk that connects a leaf blade to the stem. It is a good area to examine for mites and for the first symptoms.
Preventive / curativeA preventive product is a product that you apply before a problem starts, to keep the pressure low. A curative product is a product that you apply after you confirm the problem, to decrease the problem.
PredatorA beneficial organism that eats many prey organisms during its life cycle.
PupaThe stage between larva and adult in insects with complete metamorphosis. It does not usually eat. It is frequently in the media or in debris, where a spray does not touch it.
PycnidiumA small fruiting body of a fungus, with the shape of a flask, that makes conidia. In a lesion of Septoria it is a dark point in the middle of the lesion.
REIRestricted entry interval. The period after an application. In this period, no person can go into the area where you applied the product. A person can go into the area only if the person uses the specified PPE and obeys the specified conditions.
ReleaseThe quality decision, with a record, that lets you move or supply a plant, material or batch that you hold. You make this decision when the necessary evidence and approvals are complete.
Residue limitThe maximum limit or action limit of a residue for the release of a batch. The residue must be less than the limit. A limit for a substance is not approval to apply the substance.
Rogue / roguingTo remove a plant with a disease or pests from the production system and to destroy it.
RussetingRough tissue with a bronze-brown color that injury from pests that eat the plant, or other stress, causes.
SeverityHow much of one plant or one site has the problem. You record severity and incidence as two different values. Incidence is the number of plants or sites that have the problem.
SachetA small bag that releases beneficial organisms into the crop gradually, if the necessary conditions are correct.
SanitationThe task to clean and, when it is necessary, to disinfect a specified surface or system with a validated method. The method removes debris and decreases the quantity of live contamination.
ScoutingAn inspection of the plants on a route on the map, which you do again and again. You use it to find, identify and count pests and diseases, and to monitor the trend of the condition of the crop.
Sooty moldA dark fungus on honeydew. It shows that insects that eat sap are above it. It is not usually the primary pathogen of the plant.
SprenchAn application with a volume between a spray and a drench. You apply it to the bottom of the plant and to the surface of the media.
SporeA fungus, or an organism with some of the properties of a fungus, makes spores. A spore lets the organism increase in number or survive. The type of spore changes how you collect samples and how you control the spores. Debris between crops is important, because spores can stay in it.
StipplingThe many very small pale points that stay where mites, or pests of the same type, remove the content of cells one by one.
Systemic resistanceA condition in all the plant that a stimulus starts. It can make the plant more resistant to a pathogen. It is not evidence of control of a pathogen that is in the plant.
VectorEach item or organism that moves a pest or pathogen from one area to a different area. A vector can be an insect, a tool, or the hands of a person.
ViroidA small RNA molecule that can infect a plant, with no protein shell. A viroid has a smaller number of parts than a virus. HLVd is a viroid.
ZoosporeA spore that moves in water. Oomycetes, for example Pythium, make zoospores. Thus the disease moves through a water system.
ACVMThe New Zealand regulations for agricultural compounds and veterinary medicines. MPI controls the regulations.
EPAThe Environmental Protection Authority of New Zealand. It sets the approvals and controls for dangerous substances and new organisms.
HSNOThe regulations of the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act. The EPA controls the regulations.
Inhalation / non-inhalation lineThe decision if a lot can become a product that a patient inhales. The decision changes which regulations for the active ingredients of pesticides apply to the lot, and it changes the consequences for quality. You must make the decision before you select an input.
MPIThe Ministry for Primary Industries of New Zealand. It controls ACVM and the biosecurity pathways for inputs and for imported organisms.
Minimum quality standardThe quality requirements in the regulations that medicinal cannabis materials and products must obey before you supply them in New Zealand.
Approved-input registerThe controlled list of the products and organisms that the site accepts. For each item, the site examines the regulations, the safety, the test methods, the compatibility and the operations before it accepts the item.
Batch holdA quality status that stops the movement or release of a batch while the evidence or an investigation is not complete.
Trade wasteLiquid waste from commercial sites or industrial sites that goes into the wastewater network, with the controls of Watercare and of Auckland.

03 · System

Basic information about IPM, control layers and the feedback loop

IPM is a loop with these steps: prevent the movement of pests into the facility, monitor with the same procedure each time, and identify correctly. Then compare the finding with a controlled threshold, use compatible controls together, record the result and do a recheck. If the recheck shows that the result is not satisfactory, the loop starts again with a stronger response. The reference on arthropods and the cannabis literature agree with control in layers for an indoor crop, and not with one product on a calendar[11].

The table shows the control layers in the correct sequence for the operation. Chemical products and low-risk products are in the last layer. They can have the correct effect, but the regulations and the compatibility give them more limits than the products of the other layers.

Environmental controlRemove the conditions of temperature, moisture, airflow and root zone that help the problem.Use this layer before you use a higher layer.
Biological controlRelease the correct predator, parasitoid, nematode or antagonist before the pest pressure becomes too high. The number of predators increases slowly. 04 · New Zealand legal gate

The legal gate for IPM controls in NZ

In New Zealand, no one document has all the regulations for pests in medicinal cannabis. The decision uses these sources of regulations: the regulations for medicinal cannabis and the minimum quality standard, ACVM, and the controls of HSNO and EPA. It also uses WorkSafe, the release of batches after laboratory tests, and the requirements of Auckland for trade waste and for the environment.

Regulation 18 has limits on the treatment of cannabis crops with pesticides. Regulation 7 gives the residues for which a test is necessary and the limits for these residues. The current guidance of the Ministry shows the difference between inhalation pathways and non-inhalation pathways. The guidance also clearly keeps the requirements of ACVM and HSNO[1][2]. A residue panel can include abamectin, spinosad, pyrethrins or a different analyte. A substance in the panel does not show that the regulations let you apply it.

For most agricultural compounds, ACVM registration is necessary. Some classes of product have an exemption, but you must obey the conditions of the exemption and the other regulations[3]. You must make sure that the approvals and the controls of the EPA are correct. This check includes the approval information in section 15 of the current New Zealand SDS[5].

  1. 1
    1. End product of the lot
    Before all other steps, make the decision if a part of this lot can become flower that a patient inhales. If it can, keep the lot on the inhalation pathway from the start. The regulations for products that a patient does not inhale let you use more substances. It is easy to use these regulations accidentally. These regulations are not available to a lot that a patient can inhale.
  2. 2
    2. Medicinal cannabis pathway
    Make sure that the active ingredient or product has a route that the regulations accept specially for medicinal cannabis. Regulation 18 is an example of this route. Regulation 7 is for residues that a laboratory measures in the end product. A substance can be in this regulation because a laboratory measures it. The regulation does not give approval to apply the substance to the crop.
  3. 3
    3. ACVM authority
    Make sure that the product has a registration. If it does not, make sure that an exemption includes the product. Make sure that a document shows the exemption, and obey each condition of the exemption. If a product is not on a register, you do not know its status. This fact does not show that the product has an exemption.
  4. 4
    4. HSNO approval
    Make sure that the substance has an EPA approval, and read the controls of the approval. The safety data sheet (SDS) is the hazard document of the supplier for a product. Examine section 15 of the current New Zealand version, and compare it with the EPA database. Do not use only the sheet.
  5. 5
    5. Worker controls
    Read the current label and the safety data sheet. They give the necessary personal protective equipment (PPE) and the controls for the application. The restricted entry interval (REI) is the period after an application. In this period, a person can go into the area only with the specified PPE and conditions. The product sets the length of the period, and the crop area can look correct before the period ends. An indoor area with an REI must have signs and controlled access.
  6. 6
    6. Laboratory test route
    Before you put a product in the approved-input register, make sure that you know four items. Find which residues the laboratory must measure and the limit of residues for the release of the batch. Make sure that the accreditation of the laboratory includes the test for these residues. Make sure that the method can measure a sufficiently low concentration. The lowest concentration that a method can measure with satisfactory confidence is the limit of quantification (LOQ). A method with an LOQ more than the limit for release cannot show that the batch is correct.
  7. 7
    7. Compatible with beneficial organisms
    Examine the current side-effect data for each beneficial organism in the room. The beneficial organisms are predatory mites, predator insects, parasitoids, nematodes and microbe products. Side-effect data show the effect of a product on these organisms and the time that the effect continues. A spray can obey the regulations and also kill the beneficial organisms. The number of these organisms increased slowly, during many weeks.
  8. 8
    8. Route to the environment
    Before the application, make a decision on where each of these items goes and how you contain it. The items are the remaining mixture, rinse water, PPE with contamination and spilled liquid. No item goes to stormwater. The position of Watercare for the site sets if an item can go to the sewer as trade waste.
Do not write SKUs, rates, PHIs or REIs in a general paper

Record these values in the approved-input register with the current label and SDS. Use version control for the register. WorkSafe tells you that the REI is different for each product, crop, application and exposure. For an application that is not on the label, a risk assessment for that application is necessary. An indoor area with an REI must have warning signs and controlled access[6].

05 · Exclusion

Clean-stock controls in IPM

The mother room is not only the area where clones start. It is a system for starting material. A problem in the mother room increases in each cutting, room and batch downstream.

A plant with HLVd can show no symptoms. HLVd moves easily with vegetative propagation and with tools that have contamination. Investigations show a risk that HLVd can move through roots and through recirculating hydroponic solution[24][25][26]. Thus a visual check of the plant is not a test for release.

  • Make foundation mothers only from released material. Foundation mothers have the best controls.
  • You can trace the parent of each production mother, cutting lot and room.
  • Sanitize tools between specified groups of plants. Do not sanitize tools only at the end of the work period.
  • Quarantine, foundation stock and production stock do not use the same nutrient solution. They do not use the same return water without validation.
  • Before you collect the first sample, write the procedure for a positive result or an inconclusive result. The procedure must show how to hold the plants, do the test again, destroy the plants and do the traceback.

06 · Contamination pathways

Facility contamination pathways

A vector can move a pest or a pathogen from one area to a different area. It is not important which group of personnel has the vector. A clean-stock program gives no protection if a worker goes back to a clean area or if scissors go between mothers. It also gives no protection if return air connects to quarantine or if a reservoir for more than one area moves root pathogens. References on cannabis diseases frequently show these routes of contamination: stock, tools, water, debris, density and the conditions of the environment. The routes have an effect on each other[19].

Personnel
Use gowning for each room class. Move from clean areas to dirty areas in each work period. Do not go back to a clean area without a record. Use controls for the treated areas. Keep training records for the site.
Tools
Keep tools for one room or one plant class only. Make sure that the sanitizer concentration and the contact time are correct. Sanitize tools between groups of plants. Make it easy to know if a tool is clean or dirty.
Air
Isolate quarantine from the other areas. Set the difference of pressure between rooms. Use filtered supply air. Do not use the same return air for an area with contamination and for a clean area. Make a map of the air movement in the canopy and of the dead zones. Do checks for condensation.
Water
Where the consequence of a problem is high, use dedicated tanks and circuits. Do not use recirculating water without validation. Use biofilm control. Make a map of the drains. Prevent backflow.
Plants and materials
Use approved sources. Seal waste. Use clean media and clean pots. Use controls for the beneficial organisms that you receive. Do not let cardboard or packaging go into clean rooms.
Waste
Put crop waste in bags in the room. Record the waste. Contain rinse water and spill liquids. Use approved disposal pathways and trade waste pathways. Do not send waste to stormwater.

A trade waste agreement with Watercare is necessary if the discharge of a facility is not low risk. The trade waste agreement gives the controls and the monitoring for the site[8]. In the E33 requirements of Auckland, the primary task is to prevent the discharge of contaminants. The requirements also make it necessary to have the correct control on the site, containment, treatment or permitted disposal[9]. The address of the site, the drainage and the classification of the operation are data that the facility must supply.

07 · Prevention

Cultural controls and environmental controls

These controls continue to operate when the facility becomes larger. Remove weeds, algae and plant debris. Keep doors, screens and barriers in good condition. Put each source of genetics in quarantine. Move personnel and material in one direction.

Keep the same scouting route. Measure the conditions of the root zone and of the canopy. Set the conditions correctly. These controls decrease the risk that pests go into the facility, and the rate of movement of pests in the facility.

A prevention check must give a result that you can see, which is satisfactory or unsatisfactory. A general instruction to keep the room clean is not a check.

1. Rice root aphid (Rhopalosiphum rufiabdominale)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe signs are chlorosis with no known cause, slow growth and a weak response to irrigation. Other signs are tacky material or wax at the root crown, and colonies on the roots or at the bottom of the pot. Each symptom above the media can also have other causes.
Life cycle and movementColonies of aphids without wings eat the roots below the surface of the media. When a colony has too many aphids, it makes aphids with wings. These aphids go away and start new colonies. The aphids move in cuttings with roots, loose media, pots and runoff water. They also move on the hands and tools that touch these items.
Development and the conditions that change itIn root-zone conditions that are good for the aphids, one full generation can be approximately 10 to 14 days. The plant, the temperature and the media change this period. Look for the three types of aphid: the live young, the adults without wings, and the adults with wings. The adults with wings go to new plants.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. You can see colonies on a root mass without magnification when you remove the media. To confirm the organism, use a hand lens or a microscope at approximately 30x to 60x. At this magnification you can see clearly the antennae, the cornicles (the pair of tubes near the rear) and the body shape of an adult with wings.
Where to lookLift the material that is on the root zone. Examine the crown, the outer edge of the root mass, the drainage holes and the debris below the bench. Put yellow traps immediately above the surface of the media. Keep each specimen with wings that you cannot identify.
Identification checkMicroscopy, or identification by a specialist. Compare the antennae, the cornicles and the body shape with an adult fungus gnat before you select a control.
Problems with the same signsAdult fungus gnats, stress in the root zone or from nutrients, Pythium root rot, and too much irrigation.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine, in foundation mother plants, in production mother plants and in clone rooms, the tolerance is zero. One confirmed colony in production starts containment and an inspection of each lot that connects to it.
First responseIsolate the zone and put the plants with the most damage in bags. Do not move pots or media to a different area. Examine the mother plants and the clone lots that connect to this lot. Remove standing water and algae, but do not dry the roots until they have stress. Select only beneficial organisms or inputs for the root zone that the New Zealand legal gate accepts at this time. Then do a recheck of the roots and the low traps at the approved interval.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock and media that have pests, keep pots apart, and clean spilled liquid. Environmental control: remove standing water and algae, but do not cause stress to the roots. Biological control: you can use predators or nematodes for the root zone that the New Zealand regulations accept. Select the predators or nematodes that agree with the life stage that is the target. Approved input: use only a product that the New Zealand legal gate accepts, with a registration or an exemption for the root zone.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the colonies that you can see and the nymphs that move on the roots and the crown. Monitor the adults with wings on the traps immediately above the media. A spray on the canopy does not touch a colony below the surface.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityIn quarantine and in mother plants, the tolerance stays at zero. Before you apply a treatment, examine the legal status of the organism or product. Make sure that this status includes use in the root zone. Make sure that the treatment does not kill the beneficial organisms in the room. Then examine the crop stage, the PPE and REI on the label, and the effect of the residue on testing.
How to collect and keep the specimenPut a root sample or crown sample, with a label and some live colony on it, in a bag. Keep the specimens with wings from the low traps in a different container. Record the plant, lot, room, root zone and time of sampling. Keep the chain of custody complete. The chain of custody is a written record, with no gaps, of the persons who held the sample and the time.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultExamine the roots at the points on the map and the low traps again after the approved interval. Do this again after one full generation. The result is good when there is no live colony, no new adult with wings, and no movement to connected plants.
Source trace and CAPA triggerTrace the source genetics and the lots of media and pots. Trace the connections of irrigation and runoff, the benches, the tools, and the movement of personnel. Hold the connected clone lots until you know the source of the problem and how far it went.

2. Fungus gnats (Bradysia species)

First signs that show the problem correctlyAdults walk on wet media, or they move slowly in the air above it. Larvae are where there are algae, roots that die and slime films. Larvae eat root hairs, and the damage makes the growth of young plants slow. The wounds on the roots can have an effect on root disease.
Life cycle and movementThe eggs and larvae are in the media. The larva becomes a pupa in the media, and only the adult moves in the air. A spray on the canopy does not touch most of the gnats.
Development and the conditions that change itThe time from egg to adult is frequently approximately 18 to 28 days. The temperature, moisture, substrate and the material that the larvae can eat change this time. Monitor each stage: the eggs, larvae, pupae and adults. A trap count measures the adults only, and does not measure all the stages.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Adults are approximately 2 to 4 mm (0.08 to 0.16 in) long, and you can see them easily above the media. To confirm them, use approximately 30x to 60x. At this magnification you can see the shiny black head of a larva. You can also see the veins of the wings and the body shape of an adult.
Where to lookRead the yellow traps that you put low near the media. Put pieces of potato on the media, or collect samples of the media, to find larvae. Examine the irrigation uniformity, standing water, algae and debris from plants.
Identification checkThe veins of the wings and the body shape of an adult, or the black head capsule of a larva, with a loupe or microscope. Keep the specimens if it is possible that they are root aphids with wings.
Problems with the same signsRoot aphids with wings, shore flies, small flies that do not cause damage, and the weak growth of roots that Pythium causes.
Facility thresholdIf there is a larva in propagation, start the first response. In other areas, set a threshold for the trend at the site. A number of adults that increases on many traps is more important than one adult on one trap.
First responseDry the surface of the media as much as is safe for the crop. Remove algae and repair leaks. Clean the drains. Use an approved biological control for the larvae in the root zone. Use the traps to make sure that the number of adults decreases during the next life cycle.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: remove algae, debris and reservoirs with standing water, and repair leaks. Environmental control: let the surface dry as much as is safe for the crop, and correct drainage that is bad. Biological control: for the larvae, use nematodes or predators that stay in the media and that the New Zealand regulations accept. Approved input: keep a drench or surface treatment in its approved use, and apply it to the stage that it has an effect on.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the larva in the top part of the moist substrate and in the root zone, because the larva causes the damage. The traps show the movement of the adults. They do not control the pests. Monitor the media and the traps together during one full cycle.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityDo not let the dryback become more than the tolerance of the crop. Before you use an input, make sure that it does not kill the beneficial organisms in the root zone. Examine its effect on the irrigation system and the crop stage. Examine the PPE and REI on the label, and the residue route.
How to collect and keep the specimenSeal adults, or pieces of traps with adults, in one container with a label. Seal a sample of media or roots with larvae in a different container with a label. Record the plant, zone, height of the trap and how wet the media was. Send each specimen that you cannot identify for microscopy.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultCount the live larvae at the same points in the media. Count the adults on the same traps on the map. Do this at the interval of the site, during one full generation. The result is good when the trend decreases and stays low, and there is no root injury in propagation.
Source trace and CAPA triggerExamine the irrigation uniformity, leaks, algae and slime films, drains, new media and propagation hygiene. Examine each root disease that occurs at the same time.

3. Thrips (Frankliniella occidentalis and other thrips species)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe signs are silver areas, new leaves with an incorrect shape, scarring on flowers, and dark points. Adults and larvae stay in folded new leaves and in flowers. The stages between larva and adult are frequently in the media or in debris and not on the plant.
Life cycle and movementThe adults put the eggs in plant tissue, and a spray cannot touch the eggs there. The larvae eat the plant. The next stages do not eat. They stay in areas with protection, and then the adults go back to the canopy.
Development and the conditions that change itIn warm indoor conditions, one generation is frequently approximately 14 to 21 days. The species and the temperature change this time. The eggs are in plant tissue and the larvae eat the crop. The stages that do not eat stay in areas with protection. The adults go to other plants.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. To find adults and larvae, examine the plant parts carefully. At approximately 30x to 60x you can see the pale larvae and the hairs on the edge of the wings of the adults. At this magnification you can also identify the species and find eggs in the tissue.
Where to lookUse blue or yellow traps. Tap flowers above white paper. Examine new folded leaves and flowers carefully. Collect samples of the media and debris where the larvae become pupae.
Identification checkA loupe or microscope. The identification of the species is important when you select a specialist biological control. It is also important when you examine the risk that thrips move a virus from one plant to a different plant.
Problems with the same signsMechanical damage, damage from wind, edema, and injury from a previous spray.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine and in clean stock, the tolerance is zero. In production, one confirmed hotspot, or a trap count that increases from one reading to the next, starts the response of the site.
First responsePut the hotspot in containment. Remove the tissue that has the most damage carefully, to prevent the movement of insects across the room. Apply a treatment to the canopy and to the stages in areas with protection away from the plant. Make sure that the treatment does not kill the beneficial organisms in the room. Then read the traps on the map again after the recheck period ends.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock that has pests, put the material of hotspots in bags, and remove debris. Environmental control: remove weeds and debris that give protection to thrips, and correct the canopy conditions that help them. Biological control: use predators or parasitoids that the New Zealand regulations accept and that agree with the species and the stage. Approved input: select a contact product or microbe product that the regulations accept, that touches the target, and that does not kill the beneficial organisms.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the larvae that eat the new leaves and flowers, and the stages that are in areas with protection away from the plant. The adults move, and the eggs are in the tissue. Thus one contact application is not sufficient. Set the monitoring and the time of the next application from these facts.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityThe species can change the biological control that works. Contact with flowers and residues that cause damage to beneficial organisms limit the selection. The quality at the crop stage, the PPE and REI on the label, and the effect of the residue on testing also limit the selection.
How to collect and keep the specimenCollect larvae and adults from a hotspot on the map. Put them in vials with a label, and seal the vials. Keep a leaf or flower with symptoms in a different container. Record the trap and the plant part that each specimen came from. Thus the person who identifies the specimen has the information about the source.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultTap the flowers above white paper again, examine the plants again, and count the traps again. Do this at the approved interval, during one full generation. The result is good when there are no larvae that eat the plant and the number of adults decreases. The problem must not move out of the first hotspot.
Source trace and CAPA triggerTrace the new plants, cut flowers and packaging, doors and screens, weeds and tools. Trace the sequence in which personnel move through rooms, and the debris in the substrate. Identify the species if the control gives a bad result or if the virus risk is different for each species.

4. Two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe first sign is the stippling of small pale points. Then the leaves show bronzing and deterioration, and webbing occurs on petioles, shoots and flowers. Small areas in the canopy that are hot and dry usually show the signs first.
Life cycle and movementThe stages are the egg, a larva with six legs, two nymph stages and the adult. Frequently all the stages occur at the same time. When the temperature is higher, the development is much faster. Thus a small patch can become a large problem in days.
Development and the conditions that change itThe time from egg to adult can be approximately 7 to 14 days. The time is shorter when the canopy temperature increases. Look for eggs, the larva with six legs, the two nymph stages and adults. Indoors, these stages usually occur at the same time.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Examine each leaf, on the underside, with a loupe at 10x to 30x. Use a microscope if you are not sure about the two dark patches or the eggs. Use it also to identify the difference between the pest and a mite that is a beneficial organism.
Where to lookMake a map of the hot, dry zones first. Examine the undersides of leaves with a loupe at 10x to 30x. Tap the foliage above white paper. Find the live colony that made the webbing.
Identification checkMicroscopy for the two dark patches and the life stages. Before you apply a treatment, identify the pest mites and the mites that are beneficial organisms.
Problems with the same signsResidue of a spray, small points from nutrient problems, dust, and mites that are beneficial organisms.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine and on mother plants, the threshold is one confirmed colony. In the vegetative stage and in the flowering stage, one colony that increases in number is sufficient to start containment. Do not wait for webbing.
First responseIsolate the hotspot and put a mark on it. Do not let personnel and tools move through the hotspot. Put the leaves with the most damage in bags, and correct the areas that are very hot and dry. Release the approved predatory mites, or use a different control that the regulations accept and that is compatible with them. Examine the result from the number of live mites and not from the leaf damage. The damage stays on the leaf.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock that has pests, put the leaves of the hotspot in bags, and prevent the movement of mites by contact. Environmental control: correct areas that are very hot and dry, but do not make an area with high humidity that increases the risk of disease. Biological control: release predatory mites at the first stage of the problem, if the regulations accept them at this time. Approved input: use only a compatible contact product or microbe product that the regulations accept and that touches the undersides of leaves.
Target stage and plant partThe target is live mites and eggs on the undersides of leaves, and the outer edge of the hotspot. Colonies in flowers with webbing are not easy to touch, and they cause more damage to the batch.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityStippling from before the treatment does not show if the treatment had an effect. Before you use an input, examine its effect on the predators and the contact with flowers. Examine the crop stage and the PPE and REI on the label. Examine the residue route and the risk of damage to the leaves.
How to collect and keep the specimenPut leaves with live mites from the outer edge of the hotspot in bags with a label. Keep the samples cool and complete. If the laboratory tells you to prepare a slide or a tape mount, prepare it as a different sample. Record the temperature of the hotspot and the position of the plant.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultCount the live mites and eggs again on the same leaves on the map, and at the hotspot edge. Do this after the approved interval, and again before the end of one full generation. The result is good when the problem does not move out from the hotspot and the number of live stages decreases and stays low.
Source trace and CAPA triggerTrace the contact between plants, the personnel and tools, the carts, and the new stock. Trace the hot, dry areas with no airflow. Examine the time between the start of the problem and the finding of the mites. Examine if the number of predators did not increase because of a residue that is not compatible.

5. Broad mite (Polyphagotarsonemus latus)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe new growth is hard, glossy or twisted, with blisters. The internodes are short, and the shoot tips have damage. The symptoms stay after the mites go away.
Life cycle and movementMost of the mites are on the new growth, where they have protection. They move on plant material, on workers and on equipment. The mites are very small. If you look without magnification, you do not find all the mites.
Development and the conditions that change itIn a warm crop with high humidity, one generation can be approximately 4 to 7 days. When you set the time of the controls, include all four stages. The stages are the egg, the larva with six legs, the stage between larva and adult, and the adult. The egg has points or ridges. The stage between larva and adult does not move, and it is in an area with protection. The adult is too small to see without magnification.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. The adults are less than approximately 0.2 mm (0.008 in), and you cannot see them without magnification. Examine complete shoot tips and the eggs, which have a typical shape, at approximately 20x to 60x. Use a compound microscope to confirm.
Where to lookCollect samples of the newest tissue with an incorrect shape, the bottom of the petioles, and the shoot tips. Make a tape mount or a slide with a piece of leaf, for a dissecting microscope or a compound microscope.
Identification checkMites that you see with the microscope, and the typical eggs. Confirm the identification before you change the feed or apply an emergency treatment, because the symptoms have different causes.
Problems with the same signsLeaf edges that bend because of heat or light, and injury from a herbicide or a spray. Nutrient toxicity, and slow growth that a virus or viroid causes. Fasciation, which is an incorrect shape of the meristem because of the genetics of the plant.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine, in mother plants and in propagation, the tolerance is zero. In production, each confirmed finding is very bad, because the infestation started a long time before you can see it.
First responseIsolate the confirmed plants of clean stock, and usually remove them. Trace the clones and the movements. Collect more samples of tips. Select a control that the regulations accept at this time and that agrees with the crop stage. Examine the result from the new growth and microscopy. Do not use the leaves that have an incorrect shape from before.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock that has pests, isolate the tips, and remove the confirmed plants of clean stock. Environmental control: correct too much heat and humidity, but do not use one setpoint for all rooms. Biological control: use a predator that the regulations accept at this time, with evidence that it kills the stages of the broad mite. Approved input: use only a product that the regulations accept and that touches the shoot tip. The product must be compatible with the predators and the crop stage.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the eggs and the mites that move in shoot tips, folded leaves and the bottom of petioles. The stages that do not move are in areas that a spray does not touch. Thus the result is good only if the coverage and the time of the next application are correct.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityFor the diagnosis, a microscope is necessary, because the symptoms stay after the mites go away. Do not kill the beneficial organisms. 7. Whiteflies (Trialeurodes vaporariorum, Bemisia tabaci complex and related species)

Disease diagnosis and sampling

Disease symptoms can be the same for different diseases. Use the symptoms to select the tissue, the records of the environment and the correct laboratory route. Do not make a release decision only because a symptom agrees with a photo.

The cannabis literature on diseases gives evidence for different controls for powdery mildew, Botrytis, Pythium, Fusarium and systemic pathogens in propagation[20][21][22]. Related species of Septoria make the diagnosis not easy. Thus the color of the lesion is not sufficient for the diagnosis[27][28].

1. Powdery mildew (Golovinomyces and other powdery mildew fungi on cannabis)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe signs are pale areas with a matte color. Growth and spores occur on the surface of the areas. The colonies become larger and release airborne spores. These spores start the next infections.
Life cycle and movementThe fungus is on the surface of the plant, and it gets nutrients from the cells immediately below the surface. Canopies with high density, genetics that are not resistant, and air that does not move help the fungus to move to other plants. Free water on the leaf is not necessary for this.
Development and the conditions that change itIn canopy conditions that are good for the fungus, germination starts in hours. In some days, colonies that you can see can make a new group of spores. The cultivar and the local conditions change this interval.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Examine all the leaves, stems and flowers with light from the side. Then examine one colony at approximately 30x to 60x. Use microscopy or a laboratory when it is possible that the cause is spray residue or trichomes.
Where to lookUse light from the side across the leaves and stems. Examine the bottom and inner parts of the canopy and the flowers. Record each colony on the map. Compare the finding with the records of the last sprays.
Identification checkA hand lens or microscope to see the structures of the fungus. Send the sample to a laboratory when dry spray, mineral residue, trichomes or dust make the identification not sure.
Problems with the same signsDry residue of a foliar spray, mineral residue, dust, trichomes, and the reflection of light on the leaf.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine and on mother plants, the tolerance is zero. The tolerance is also zero for one colony on a flower. One colony on a plant in the vegetative stage starts containment and more scouting in all the room.
First responsePut the tissue with the problem in bags, and do not shake it. Do not let personnel and tools move through the room. Decrease the density of the canopy and correct areas with high humidity and no airflow. Use only a method that the regulations accept and that agrees with the crop stage. Examine if new colonies occur, and do not use the scarring from before.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock with the disease, put lesions in bags, and clean the items that move spores. Environmental control: correct zones with high density and no airflow, and correct the humidity or dew point when it is not in the correct range. Biological control: use only an antagonist that prevents the disease and that the regulations accept, with evidence that it controls this pathogen. Approved input: select a product that the regulations accept, that touches the crop, and that is correct for the tissue and the growth stage.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the first colonies on the surface and each new movement of the fungus on leaves and stems. When a flower has an infection, it is a quality event. Then only a small number of methods are possible.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityDo not think that a residue is an infection. Contact with flowers, residues that you can see, and the compatibility with beneficial organisms limit the selection of an input. The PPE and REI on the label, the crop stage, the risk of damage to the leaves, and the testing for release also limit it.
How to collect and keep the specimenMake a photo of a complete colony. Use clean tools to put the colony in a bag with some adjacent tissue that is in good condition. Keep the records of the last sprays with the sample. Send the sample dry and cool, as the instruction of the laboratory tells you.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultGo back to the points on the map after the approved interval. Use light from the side and microscopy. Do this again in the period when a new group of spores can occur. The result is good when there are no new colonies and the colonies that you found before do not become larger.
Source trace and CAPA triggerExamine the source stock, the personnel and tools, the airflow and the canopy density. Examine the humidity and dew point when the lights are off. Examine how resistant the cultivar is, and the flowers and batches that have the problem.

2. Botrytis bud rot (Botrytis cinerea)

First signs that show the problem correctlyOne leaf wilts. Soft or dry necrosis occurs in a flower with high density, and gray mold occurs. In conditions with high humidity, the disease moves quickly into tissue that touches the infection.
Life cycle and movementSpores go into tissue that is not resistant, that has a wound, or that is in senescence. Flowers with high density keep air with high humidity in them. Sclerotia are hard survival bodies of this fungus. Sclerotia and debris with the infection can move the fungus from one crop to the next crop.
Development and the conditions that change itWhen the humidity is high for a long time and the tissue is not resistant, an infection can cause decay and spore growth. You can see them in approximately 3 to 5 days. The temperature, the time that the tissue is wet, wounds, and the shape of the flower change the rate.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. First examine all the flower. Then open the flower, and examine the edge of the lesion and the growth of spores with a hand lens. Use laboratory microscopy or culture when the species has an effect on the decision about the batch.
Where to lookExamine the flowers with a high risk each day. Open each bud with high density that you think has a problem. Record the cultivar, the zone, the irrigation events, the humidity at night, and the mechanical injuries.
Identification checkThe typical decay and spore growth in the inner part of the flower. Use a diagnostic laboratory if the decision about the batch changes with the species.
Problems with the same signsCaterpillar damage, mechanical damage, usual senescence, and other flower rots.
Facility thresholdEach confirmed flower is immediately a containment event, with consequences for the batch.
First responseStop movement, and stop the fans that can move spores while you remove the material. Put the infected tissue in a bag, with some tissue in good condition around it. Examine the plants near it, and hold the lot with the problem as the procedure of the site tells you. Correct the humidity, the airflow and the canopy density. Open a CAPA if there is more than one finding.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: remove tissue in senescence and tissue with wounds, and put flowers with the problem in bags. Environmental control: prevent condensation, tissue that stays wet for a long time, and zones with high density and no airflow in the canopy. Biological control: use only an antagonist that prevents the disease and that the regulations accept, with evidence. Approved input: in the last stage of flowering, a product must have an approval for the regulations, the quality and the residues.
Target stage and plant partThe target is to prevent the infection of tissue that has wounds or is in senescence, and to remove the lesions in the flower. A spray on the surface does not repair necrosis in the flower.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityA flower with the problem has consequences for the batch and for its microbial quality. Touch the material carefully, to prevent the release of spores. Before you use an input, examine the crop stage and the PPE and REI on the label for the workers. Examine also the residues, the compatibility and the testing for release.
How to collect and keep the specimenStop the local airflow, and make a photo of the flower in its position. Use clean tools to put the edge of the lesion in a bag. Also put a flower that you did not open, and that you think has the problem, in a bag. Keep the bags cool. Do not send the bags with the samples of flowers in good condition that you use to compare.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultExamine the flowers on the map each day during containment. Open flowers again at the approved interval, to examine the inner part. The result is good when there is no new decay or spore growth in the zone with the problem.
Source trace and CAPA triggerExamine the cultivar, the structure of the flower and the injury. Examine the canopy density, the humidity and dew point when the lights are off, and the airflow. Examine the sanitation, the personnel and tools, and each lot or batch with the problem.

3. Pythium root, crown rot and damping-off (Pythium species (oomycetes))

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe signs are weak roots, wilt in the middle of the day although the media is wet, chlorosis, and slow uptake of water. Other signs are collapse at the bottom of the stem, and the same symptoms on all the plants that use the same water circuit.
Life cycle and movementSpores with thick walls, for survival, stay in the system. The organism also makes zoospores (spores that move in water). Warm root zones, low dissolved oxygen, saturation and slime films can increase the risk. The species are different.
Development and the conditions that change itZoospores can infect root tips that are not resistant quickly. Symptoms can increase in some days in systems that are warm, that have low oxygen, or that have saturation. The species, the age of the plant and the root-zone conditions change the development.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Compare the root mass and the root tips with white roots in good condition. Compare also the outer layer of the roots and the crown. To identify the cause, you must use a diagnostic laboratory: microscopy, culture or molecular testing.
Where to lookExamine the roots, the crown, the drain water, and the slime film in tanks and pipes. Measure the root-zone temperature and the dissolved oxygen. Examine the frequency of irrigation, the standing water, and each group of plants that uses the same water.
Identification checkA diagnostic laboratory for plants, with microscopy, culture or molecular testing. The color of the roots is not sufficient to identify the organism.
Problems with the same signsRoots that die because of causes that are not organisms. Low oxygen with no infection, Fusarium, and injury from salt in the nutrients. The usual brown color of roots in senescence.
Facility thresholdThe threshold is one group of seedlings that collapse, one crown rot, or a pattern on plants that use the same water circuit. Then isolate the plants and examine the water system.
First responseDo not use the same water or material for different plants. Remove the plants that collapse. Collect the samples before you sanitize, because the sanitation removes the evidence. Correct the faults of oxygen, moisture and temperature in the root zone. Clean the slime films with a validated procedure. Use only beneficial organisms or inputs that the regulations accept.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: start with clean stock and clean media, and isolate the water circuits with the problem. Environmental control: correct saturation, heat, low dissolved oxygen, standing water and slime films. Biological control: use only an antagonist that prevents the disease, that the regulations accept and that is correct for the reservoir. Approved input: a product that touches the crop or roots is different from a validated sanitation of pipes or surfaces. Make sure that you know the difference.
Target stage and plant partThe target is to prevent the infection at the root tips and crowns, and to prevent the movement of the spores in the water. Collect samples before the sanitation. Remove the roots that die and correct the system. No treatment repairs a dead root.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityMany sanitizers kill the antagonists that you use. Make sure that the regulations accept the product in the root zone. Examine the status of the strain or product, and the crop stage. Examine the PPE and REI for the workers and the residue route. Examine the compatibility with the reservoir and where the waste water goes.
How to collect and keep the specimenCollect the samples before you clean. Collect roots and crown from the edge of the lesion, media, and water from the connected tank and drain pipe. Put each sample in a different sterilized container with a label. Keep the samples cool. Record the circuit and the lot that each sample came from.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultExamine the root tips, the crowns and the connected groups of plants again. Do the validated test of the water or the roots again after the approved interval. The result is good when the new roots are white and in good condition, and no more plants collapse. The problem must not move to connected plants.
Source trace and CAPA triggerExamine the source of propagation and the water circuit. Examine the temperature, the dissolved oxygen, the saturation and the dryback. Examine the slime films, the hoses and trays, the tools, the personnel, and each connected group of plants.

4. Fusarium wilt, root and crown disease (Fusarium species complexes)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe signs are weak roots, seedlings that collapse, and lesions at the bottom of the stem. Other signs are chlorosis, wilt that does not recover, and patterns that agree with a batch of stock, tools, media or water.
Life cycle and movementDifferent groups of Fusarium species cause disease in the roots, the crown, the xylem or the flowers. Spores move in water with contamination, in plant tissue and in equipment. The spores germinate at root tips or wounds. The fungus moves up in the xylem. Chlamydospores, which are spores with thick walls for survival, stay in debris of plants and media.
Development and the conditions that change itRoot infection and internal symptoms can occur in days to weeks. The group of species, the quantity of inoculum, the temperature, the moisture and the stress of the plant change this time. The spores for survival stay for a much longer time.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Examine all the plant for deterioration on one side. Then cut the crown or stem along its length, and examine the edges of the lesions on the roots. To identify the organism, you must use laboratory isolation and molecular testing.
Where to lookCut a crown or stem with symptoms along its length. Compare the internal tissue with a plant in good condition. Examine the roots. Trace the source of propagation, the tools, the lots of media and the connections of irrigation.
Identification checkLaboratory isolation, and molecular testing when it is necessary. The next steps are different for each type of disease and each source.
Problems with the same signsPythium, drought or low oxygen, mechanical injury to the roots, nutrient problems, and the weak vigor that occurs with hop latent viroid.
Facility thresholdEach confirmed finding in clean stock or in a clone line has a high consequence. Think that a group of findings in production is systemic until the trace-back shows that it is not.
First responseIsolate the plants, put them in bags and destroy them as the procedure of the site tells you. Hold the connected clones. Collect samples before you clean. Examine the stock, the tools, the media and the water. Correct the structural faults that you find. Do not tell a person that there is a cure for a plant with the fungus in the internal tissue.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: use clean media, clean tools and stock with a negative result in a test. Destroy plants with the disease in the xylem. Environmental control: correct root injury, saturation and heat, but do not think that the climate removes an infection. Biological control: use only an antagonist that prevents the disease and that the regulations accept, with evidence for that strain. Approved input: a product that you use on roots to prevent disease is different from the sanitation of pipes and surfaces. Do not mix them.
Target stage and plant partThe target is to prevent entry at the root tips and wounds, and to prevent the movement of spores between plants. When the fungus is in the xylem, there is no cure with evidence for it. Thus the decisions are about the confirmed plants and the pathways that gave the fungus to them.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityYou must confirm the species and the type of disease. Before you use an input, make sure that the sanitizer does not kill the antagonist. Make sure that the regulations accept the product in the root zone. Examine the crop stage. Examine the PPE and REI on the label, the residue route and the waste water.
How to collect and keep the specimenUse clean tools to collect the edges of the lesions on roots and crown. Collect also a section of stem along its length, with brown tissue and tissue in good condition in it. Put the pieces in different bags, keep them cool, and record the connections to the stock, media and water.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultExamine the connected groups of plants for new wilt and brown color in the stems. Do the laboratory tests again at the approved interval. The result is good when there are no new findings after you remove the plants and correct the faults. A plant with the infection that recovers does not show a good result.
Source trace and CAPA triggerTrace the genealogy of the mother plants and clones, and the source of the seeds or plants. Trace the tools, the lots of media, the water circuits, and the root injury. Trace the movement of personnel, and each connected group of plants or batch.

5. Hop latent viroid disease (Hop latent viroid (HLVd))

First signs that show the problem correctlyA clone line, or a connected hydroponic group, is weaker than usual, and you cannot find a cause in the environment. The symptoms are the same as the symptoms of many other problems. If a plant has no symptoms, this fact does not show that the plant is clean.
Life cycle and movementThe viroid moves in vegetative material with the infection and on tools with contamination. Tests also give evidence that the viroid can move through roots and through recirculating hydroponic solution.
Development and the conditions that change itA latent infection is in the plant and can move to other plants while the plant looks usual. HLVd is latent. There is no period when you can be sure to see the infection. Thus the validated method of molecular testing and the risk interval for that stock set the time of the surveillance.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Compare all the plants and the groups of clones only to select the plants for the samples. Confirm the identification with RT-qPCR or RT-PCR. Use one plant that you can trace, or a validated sample with tissue of more than one plant. Magnification does not confirm the identification.
Where to lookDo surveillance with molecular testing in quarantine, foundation mother plants and production mother plants. Make sure that you can trace each result. The validated laboratory method tells you which tissue to collect and when to collect it.
Identification checkRT-qPCR or RT-PCR only, with the correct controls. Write a procedure for tests that you do again and for inconclusive results. The AI photo is not evidence for the diagnosis.
Problems with the same signsRoot disease, irrigation faults, nutrient problems, russet mites and broad mites. Plants that are weak because of the genetics, and other pathogens that move through all the plant.
Facility thresholdIn quarantine, in foundation mother plants, in production mother plants and in the source material of clones, the tolerance is zero.
First responsePut the plant, and all the items connected to it, in quarantine, and do not move clones. Confirm the result. Destroy the plants with a positive result with the approved procedure. Hold each clone that you cut from the plant since the last confirmed negative result. Examine the tools, the route of the personnel, and each hydroponic system that connects the plants. Do follow-up tests to make sure that the corrective action had an effect.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: accept only stock with a negative result in a test, and keep the genealogies apart. Sanitize the tools between groups of plants, and destroy the plants with a confirmed positive result. Environmental control: keep recirculating solution that you did not validate apart, because the climate does not remove an infection. Biological control: no beneficial organism cures HLVd. Approved input: apply sanitizers only with a validated procedure that the regulations accept, on tools and surfaces.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the pathways, not the symptoms that you can see. The pathways are exclusion, mechanical movement and contact of roots. Monitor quarantine, foundation mother plants and production mother plants with molecular testing, and trace each lot of clones.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityA photo, a number for the vigor, or no symptoms is not sufficient to release stock. The validated RT-qPCR or RT-PCR method sets the tissue to collect and the time to collect it. It also sets the controls, the pooling, the tests that you do again, and the procedure for inconclusive results. Examine the PPE, the REI and the residues of the sanitizers in a different step.
How to collect and keep the specimenCollect the validated tissue in the container of the laboratory. Use a new tool for each plant, or disinfect the tool. Keep the chain of custody complete. Record the accession number, the plant, the mother plant and the lot of clones. Record also the person who collects the sample, the time and the temperature.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultDo the tests again for positive results and inconclusive results. Do a test of the connected material with the approved method. The result is good when you destroy the plants or put them in containment, with a record. The surveillance must give confirmed negative results in all the trace group. Symptoms that become less are not a good result.
Source trace and CAPA triggerTrace each clone that you cut since the last confirmed negative result. Trace the tools, the route of the personnel, the contact of roots, and the hydroponic circuits that plants use together. Examine if the sampling was correct. Hold the lots with the problem until quality assurance completes the investigation.

6. Septoria leaf spot (Septoria cannabis, S. neocannabina, S. cannabicola and other Septoria species)

First signs that show the problem correctlyThe bottom leaves and the leaves in senescence usually show it first. Splash of water, wet foliage and debris with the infection help the disease to move. How important the disease is indoors is different for each site.
Life cycle and movementSeptoria species make fruiting bodies and spores in the lesions. The shape and color of a lesion are not sufficient to be sure of the species name.
Development and the conditions that change itLesions and fruiting bodies occur in days to weeks. The species, the temperature and the time that the leaf stays wet change this time. The cycles that splash causes continue while wet debris stays.
Size and magnification for inspectionThe photo does not show the correct sizes. Examine all the bottom leaf. Then examine the middle of each lesion at approximately 30x to 60x, for the small dark fruiting bodies. Laboratory microscopy, culture or sequencing is mandatory when the species is important.
Where to lookMake a map of the lesions on the bottom leaves. Look for splash, wet leaves and debris with the infection. Make photos of the two sides of the leaf. Examine the records of sprays and nutrients.
Identification checkA diagnostic laboratory for plants, with microscopy or culture. Use sequencing methods when the species is important.
Problems with the same signsNutrient deficiency, splash of chemicals, Cercospora, Bipolaris and other leaf spots, mechanical injury, and usual senescence.
Facility thresholdEach confirmed finding in quarantine or in stock starts exclusion. In production, lesions that occur again and again, or a pattern that moves to other plants, start containment and laboratory work.
First responsePut the debris with the infection in bags. Stop the splash, and do not let leaves stay wet. Increase the airflow. Do not move wet tools and clothing between rooms. Confirm the diagnosis. Apply a control only if the regulations accept it, if there is evidence for it, and if it is necessary.
Control methods for each layerCultural control: reject stock with the disease, put lesions and debris in bags, and do not move wet tools between rooms. Environmental control: stop splash and leaves that stay wet for a long time, and increase the airflow at the bottom of the canopy. Biological control: use only an antagonist that the regulations accept and that has evidence for the confirmed diagnosis. Approved input: select a product that touches the crop and that the regulations accept, only after the laboratory result. Also examine if the disease is important indoors at your site.
Target stage and plant partThe target is the bottom leaves with the infection, the debris and the pathways of splash, before the lesions connect to each other. Prevent the infection of the new foliage. The necrosis in lesions does not recover.
Limits from the crop, the workers, the residue and compatibilityHow important the disease is indoors is different for each site. The species is also different for each site. Contact with flowers, the crop stage and the compatibility with beneficial organisms limit the treatment. The PPE and REI on the label, the residues, and the risk of damage to the leaves also limit the treatment. Spray injury can look the same as the disease.
How to collect and keep the specimenMake photos of the two surfaces of the leaf. Collect some dry leaves from the edges of lesions with fruiting bodies, and a leaf in good condition to compare. Put the leaves in different paper bags with a label, or in containers that the laboratory accepts. Keep the records of sprays and nutrients with them.
Recheck date and the condition for a good resultCount the new lesions on the bottom leaves and on the new leaves again, and record them on the map. Do this after the approved interval and after each event when the leaves are wet. The result is good when there are no new lesions and the disease does not move up the plant in the period of the recheck.
Source trace and CAPA triggerExamine the new stock, the debris with the infection, and the splash and irrigation from above. Examine the condensation, the wet leaves, the tools and clothing, the injury from nutrients or chemicals, and the connected rooms.

13 · Operation

The IPM program for each week

  1. 1
    Calculate the consequence
    Room class, clean-stock status, target organism, crop stage and the consequence for product quality.
  2. 2
    Measure the problem
    Incidence, severity 0-4 and life stages. The pattern in the room and the trend of traps, roots and laboratory results. The density of beneficial organisms.
  3. 3
    Zero-tolerance findings
    A zero-tolerance finding does not use a threshold with a number. Start containment immediately.
  4. 4
    Find the source
    Received stock, movement of personnel and tools, air, water, media, packaging, weeds, algae, or pests from the previous crop.
  5. 5
    Select layers
    First do a correction with cultural controls and environmental controls. Then select biological controls and permitted products that are compatible.
  6. 6
    Schedule
    Select the target life stage and the date of application or release. Select the room controls, the change of mode, the recheck date, and the instruction to stop or to escalate.
  7. 7
    Examine the result
    Measure living organisms, new lesions, new growth, establishment and damage to the plants. Examine the effect on residue and if the problem occurs again.
  8. 8
    Stop or CAPA
    Stop the response only when the result agrees with the condition for a good result. If it does not, examine the cause again and escalate.
