Dissolve Athena Pro Line powder into a 50 L stock tankDissolve Athena Pro Line powder into a 50 L stock tank

Dissolve a full 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag of Athena Pro Line in a 50 L tank to make a stock concentrate. Add a small quantity of the stock to the feed water to make the feed. This paper shows that the water temperature and the mixing are important at 227 g/L. It shows that Part A and Part B must be in different tanks. It also shows how to calculate the dose in milliliters for each liter of feed.

Purpose and scope

In this paper, the task is to dissolve a full 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag of Athena Pro Line in a 50 L tank. The result is a strong stock solution. Do not give this solution to the plants directly, because it is much too strong. Make the stock solution one time. Then measure a small quantity of the stock solution. Add it to your irrigation tank to make the feed.

A stock tank changes a powder that is not easy to use into a liquid that is easy to use. When you weigh the powder each time you make feed, the procedure is slow and the quantity is not the same each time. Dissolve the full bag one time. Then add the stock to your feed water in a quantity that you measure, in milliliters for each liter. Each time, you get the same feed in seconds.

In short

A full 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag in 50 L makes a stock of approximately 227 grams for each liter. The stock is a strong concentrate. Make sure that all the powder dissolves. Keep the two parts in different tanks. Know how many milliliters of stock to add to each liter of feed water.

Your equipment

This paper is for the equipment that you have. The equipment is a full 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag, a 50 L tank, jugs of hot water, and a paint-mixer paddle on a drill. At this concentration, you must use the paddle. You cannot mix 11 kg of salt into the solution manually.

Terms

Definitions

Stock solution (concentrate)A strong nutrient solution that you keep in a tank and add to the feed water in small quantities. In this paper, it is one full bag in 50 L. Do not give it to the plants directly.
Working solution (feed)The diluted solution that the plants use. You make it when you add a small quantity of stock to a large quantity of water.
Part A / Pro CoreThe part of Athena Pro Line that contains calcium and nitrogen. Put only Part A in its stock tank.
Part B / Pro Grow or Pro BloomThe part of Athena Pro Line that contains sulfates and phosphates. It must have a different stock tank from Part A.
Dose rateThe quantity of stock that you add to each liter of feed water, in milliliters for each liter (mL/L). You use this value each day.
Solubility / saturationThe quantity of salt that a specified volume of water can hold. Water holds more salt when the temperature is higher. If you add more salt than this limit, the salt does not dissolve and stays as grit[3]. Grit is solid particles of salt in the tank.
Precipitation / lockoutDissolved salts make a solid and fall out of the solution. For example, calcium makes a solid with sulfate or with phosphate. The nutrient is not in the water, and the plant cannot use it[2].
EC (electrical conductivity)A meter reading of the total quantity of dissolved salt. You use this reading to do a check of the strength of the feed that you make.

The primary instruction

Keep part A and part B in different tanks

Put Part A in one stock tank and Part B in a different stock tank. Do not let the two concentrates touch each other. This instruction prevents damage to your crop and to your pump. Put one 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag of Pro Core in one 50 L tank. Put one 11.34 kg (25 lb) bag of Pro Grow/Bloom in a different 50 L tank.

The cause is chemical. Part A contains a large quantity of calcium, and Part B contains sulfates and phosphates. In a diluted feed, there is no problem. But if you put the two concentrates together, the calcium immediately makes a solid with the sulfate and the phosphate. The solid is gypsum and calcium phosphate[1]. The result is a tank of sludge that you cannot use, and the crop does not get the nutrients that you added[2].

Do not do these tasks
  • Do not add Part A stock to Part B stock. Do not add Part B stock to Part A stock.
  • Do not add the two concentrates to the feed water at the same time. Add A and mix. Then add B and mix.
  • Clean the jug with water before you use the jug for the other part.
The cause

How to dissolve 227 g/L fully

227 grams of salt for each liter is a large quantity. It is approximately six to seven times more salt than in seawater. It is not automatic that all the salt dissolves. Two effects can cause some of the salt to stay solid:

  • Salt that dissolves makes the water colder. Most of these salts absorb heat when they dissolve (endothermic). Thus the tank becomes colder when you add powder, and less salt dissolves in colder water[3]. Hot water and a paddle prevent this problem.
  • Cold water and water that does not move become saturated. If you do not use heat and strong mixing, the solution is at the saturation limit. The last part of the bag stays as grit on the bottom. Then the stock has a concentration that is too low, and the concentration is not the same each time.
Hot water, not boiling water

Use hot tap water at approximately 40–50 °C (104–122 °F). Do not use boiling water. Boiling water can cause damage to some compounds. Boiling water can also burn your skin when the paddle moves the water. Warm water gives sufficient solubility.

The method

Stock-tank mixing procedure

Do the procedure below one time for each part. Do it one time for the Pro Core bag (Tank A) and one time for the Pro Grow/Bloom bag (Tank B). The steps are the same each time.

  1. 1
    Start with hot water: approximately 40 L
    Fill the 50 L tank to approximately 40 L with hot water at approximately 40–50 °C (104–122 °F). Keep headroom in the tank. The 11 kg of powder increases the volume. The water must not spill when you mix.
  2. 2
    Start the paddle before you add the powder
    Make a vortex in the water with the paddle. The salt must go into water that moves. The salt must not collect on the bottom of the tank.
  3. 3
    Add the powder slowly
    Add the powder slowly and continuously. Do not add the full bag at one time. If you add all the powder at one time, clumps occur. A clump holds dry powder in the middle, and this powder does not dissolve (a ‘fish-eye’).
  4. 4
    Mix until the solution is transparent
    Continue to mix until there is no grit and the solution is transparent. The solution can have a color. At this concentration, it is possible that you must mix for some minutes, and not for seconds.
  5. 5
    Fill to the 50 L mark
    When all the salt dissolves, add warm water until the solution is at the 50 L mark. The concentration is accurate because you add the water after the salt dissolves.
  6. 6
    Put a label and the date on the tank
    Write ‘PART A, Pro Core’ or ‘PART B, Grow/Bloom’ and the date on the label. The two tanks look the same. If you use the incorrect tank, you do not obey the primary instruction.
  7. 7
    Let the solution become colder, then do a check again
    When the solution becomes colder, look for solid material that falls out of the solution. A small quantity of sediment can show that the solution was near saturation. Mix the solution again. If the sediment stays, it is possible that you must add a small quantity of water.

To use the stock, you dilute it. The bag (or the Athena chart) gives the feed rate for a part. The feed rate is X grams for each liter of diluted feed. Use the formula below, because your stock is 226.8 g/L.

The formula for the dose rate

Dose rate (mL of stock for each L of feed) = feed rate (g/L) ÷ 226.8 × 1000 ≈ feed rate × 4.41[6]. Calculate the dose rate for Part A and for Part B. Use the feed rate of each part.

Do the last check with a meter

Mix the feed and read the EC with your EC meter. Use the meter reading and not the calculated dose rate, because water and formulas change[7]. The calculated dose rate is only an approximate value.

The feed

Feed strength targets for each stage

The stock only supplies the nutrients. The strength of the feed is important for the plant, and you read the strength as EC. A weak feed is correct for plants in the first stage, and a strong feed is correct for plants in the bulk stage. At the end of the cycle, you decrease the strength. The quantity of nutrients that cannabis uses changes during the cycle[4].

Too much feed causes stress

Do not increase the EC for more growth. A high EC does not cause more growth. The salt in the root zone removes water from the roots, and the plant cannot absorb water. This effect is osmotic stress[8]. The plant shows wilt also in a wet slab. If you are not sure, make the feed weaker by a small quantity.

Storage

Keep each stock tank sealed and away from cold and from light. Put a label on each tank. Mix the stock before you use it, because salt can fall to the bottom of the tank. Make only the quantity of stock that you will use in a short time. Do not make the supply for a year[7].

The limits

Expected results and limitations

The primary information
  1. Two bags and two tanks. Do not put the concentrates together. This instruction prevents damage to the stock.
  2. Hot water, the paddle and sufficient time make all the 226.8 g/L dissolve. The solution is transparent and has no grit.
  3. Add the stock to the feed water. Do not give the stock to the plants. Dilute the stock to an EC target. Use a meter each time to make sure of the EC.
  4. Use a feed strength that agrees with the stage, and know that a stronger feed is not better[8].

When you have the stock, the task for each day is to add the stock to the feed water and to apply the feed correctly. Read the coco and crop steering paper for information on the effect of the feed in the root zone. Read the irrigation manual for information on how to apply the feed.
