Mold risk: how to prevent and stop bud rotMold risk: how to prevent and stop bud rot

Mold is the disease with the highest risk of damage to all the crop in the last weeks. It can also make your flower dangerous to use. This paper shows the conditions that mold must have, how to prevent these conditions, and the procedure to use immediately when you find mold.

01, Read this first

Purpose and scope

You can do all the tasks correctly for ten weeks, and then in the last two weeks gray mold causes damage to all the crop. Mold increases most quickly in warm air with a high humidity and in a canopy with a high density of flowers. The same conditions also make large buds. The mold is most dangerous in the inner part of the bud. You cannot see the mold there until it increases in size.

There is also a risk to health. One set of claims data shows that persons who use cannabis have fungal infections approximately 3.5× more frequently than persons who do not use cannabis. The absolute rates are small, and persons with a weak immune system have the highest risk[8]. Some molds make mycotoxins that stay in the flower after drying[9].

The mold risk is not only about yield. It is also about safe medicine. It is not necessary to know about mold before you read this paper. The paper gives the definition of each term.

02, The terms

Definitions

Bud rot (Botrytis)Botrytis cinerea is a gray mold. It causes rot in the flowers, from the inner part to the external part. It is the primary cause of damage to the crop in the last part of flowering.
Powdery mildew (PM)A white fungus on the surface of leaves, with a layer of powder. It is a different fungus with different symptoms, but it is also a very bad problem.
Relative humidity (RH)Air can contain only a maximum quantity of water vapor, and water starts to condense on cold surfaces at this limit. RH shows how near the air is to this limit, as a percentage. An RH of 100% shows that the air is at the limit. An RH of 50% shows half of the limit. Keep the RH in flowering rooms in the range of 45–65%. When the RH is more than 70%, the risk of bud rot increases quickly.
Wet leaf / free moistureLiquid water on the plant, from condensation or from spray. Many mold spores must have this water to germinate.
Water activityDry flower also contains moisture, but mold cannot use all of this moisture. The sugars, the salts and the cell walls hold some of the moisture. This moisture is in the flower, but it cannot move. Water activity (Aw) measures only the free fraction, on a scale of 0 to 1. When Aw is less than approximately 0.65, most molds cannot increase in number. If you cure the flower and keep it in storage at an Aw less than this threshold, the risk of storage mold is very small.
MycotoxinA poison that some molds make, for example Aspergillus. It can stay in the flower, also when there is no mold[9].

Evidence and limitations

Three types of information

We want each paper to be correct. Thus we examine each paper for information that is not solid. This information comes from the view of one grower, from a small number of sources, or from grower methods that have no controlled test. We identify this information, and we do not show it as solid.

Frequently there is no paper for the decision that you must make. Then we use the reports of other growers and the methods that work in the rooms that we know. This information can help you, but it is not the result of a laboratory test. Use the methods that work for your plants, for your room and for your meters. If a table does not agree with your crop, use the data from your crop and record the difference.

Solid
Tests of plants, specifications or many sources that agree show that the information is correct
  • Botrytis occurs more frequently when the humidity is high, the tissue has a high density and the air exchange is low.
  • The RH in the canopy can be higher than the readings of the room sensors.
Grower method
Many growers use this method in their rooms. Start with it, then change it for your room.
  • Procedures to decrease the RH in steps in the last stage of flowering, and procedures to put a bag on the rot and cut it.
Weak
The view of one grower, a small number of sources, one test, or a method that works in one room
  • One RH range of 60 to 70% in flowering as a safe target for the last stage of flowering.
  • Values of relative risk from claims data, with no absolute rates. The group of persons with a weak immune system stays at high risk.

If you find an error, make a report. We will correct it. Make the report on GitHub, with the name of the paper and the information that is incorrect. If you have a source, include it: Make a report of an accuracy problem. The regulations, labels and licenses of your area always override each procedure in these papers. The label weak in a paper shows the points where the risk of an error is highest.

03, Two disease types

Frequent mold diseases of cannabis

Inner part of the bud
Bud rot, Botrytis

Bud rot starts deep in a cola with a high density, frequently at a stem or where a leaf attaches to the bud. One leaf has a yellow or brown color, and you can pull it from the bud easily. The inner part of the bud is gray and soft, and it breaks into small pieces. Bud rot increases quickly in the last weeks[1].

On the leaves
Powdery mildew, PM

PM makes white areas of powder on the top of the leaves. You can rub the leaf to remove the powder, but then the powder is on the leaf again. PM is most frequent at moderate temperatures, and in air with a high humidity that does not move. PM makes a layer on the leaves and decreases photosynthesis[3].

Other mold species in grow rooms

Cannabis in grow rooms can also have Penicillium, Cladosporium, Fusarium and Aspergillus[10]. Botrytis and PM are the two species that you will see first and most frequently.

04, Causes and conditions

Conditions that help mold

Bud rot does not occur at random. It must have a set of conditions. The humidity is more than approximately 70%, the temperatures are moderate (approximately 17–24 °C (63–75 °F)), and there is free moisture on the surfaces of the plant. The air also does not move[2]. If you remove one of these conditions, you prevent the mold.

The problem of a canopy with a high density

If the canopy has a high density and no defoliation, warm air with a high humidity stays in the canopy. The air does not move, and the microclimate is much wetter than the reading of the room sensor[1]. If you put the plants near each other to increase the yield, the risk of rot increases. Defoliation and space between the plants are good methods to control mold. They change the airflow and the humidity of the canopy.

05, Steps to prevent mold

How to prevent mold each day

To prevent mold, do a small number of easy tasks with the same procedure each time:

  1. 1
    Keep the humidity low
    Keep the RH in flowering at approximately 45–65%, with a value that is correct for the stage. In the middle and last parts of flowering, the value is frequently approximately 45–55%. Use a lower value in the last part. Select a size for the dehumidifier that is correct for the transpiration load[4].
  2. 2
    Move air through the canopy
    Make a weak airflow with turbulence. Make the air move at approximately 0.5–1.0 m/s in the plants and not only above the canopy[4]. Read the airflow paper.
  3. 3
    Open the canopy
    Defoliate the plants and make space between the plants. Thus air and light go into the canopy. A high plant density increases the other risks, and you cannot see this effect.
  4. 4
    Prevent free moisture
    Do not apply water from above and do not spray the plants in flowering. Prevent condensation at lights-off. Do not let the temperature decrease by a large quantity. Then the surface temperatures stay higher than the dew point.
  5. 5
    Keep the room clean
    Sanitize the tools, the surfaces and your hands. Use a HEPA filter on the air that comes into the room. Thus the number of spores in the air decreases[4].
  6. 6
    Scouting each day
    In the last weeks, examine the plants each day. If you find one bud with rot at the first stage, you can prevent damage to all the other buds.
06, Scouting each day

Scouting to find mold at the first stage

Each day in the last part of flowering, use a bright light for five minutes. Look into the plants and not only at the plants:

  • The first sign. One leaf blade comes out of a bud. The leaf is yellow or brown, and you can pull it from the bud easily. Pull the leaf from the bud, and examine the bud below it for gray mold.
  • Where to look. Examine the largest colas at the top, which have the highest density. Examine the positions where a leaf petiole goes into the bud. Examine all the positions where the airflow is weak.
  • Powdery mildew. White powder on the top surfaces of the leaves. You can rub the leaf to remove the powder.
  • Smell the plants. Before you can see the rot, the plants can smell of mold, smell of hay, or smell different from usual.
07, Control of damage

Procedure when you find mold

  1. 1
    Stop and contain the mold
    Do not move air onto the mold. Put a bag around the bud with rot. Cut the stem at a large distance below the rot. Remove the bag from the room before you open the bag.
  2. 2
    Cut a large area
    The area of bud rot in the bud is larger than the area that you can see. Remove the full bud with rot and some tissue in good condition around it.
  3. 3
    Sanitize
    Clean your tools and your hands before you touch a different plant. Spores can move on tools, surfaces and hands.
  4. 4
    Remove the cause
    When you find rot, the conditions of humidity, airflow or density are not correct. Decrease the RH. Make more airflow through the canopy. Open the canopy.
  5. 5
    Think about the time of harvest
    If the rot increases quickly in the last part of flowering, harvest the clean material before the usual time. This harvest can be better than damage to all the crop.
Do not try to make flower with rot safe with a ‘treatment’

A bud with rot is waste. Drying decreases the quantity of microbes, but it does not remove the rot or the mycotoxins[7]. No procedure makes the bud safe to use.

08, After harvest

How to prevent mold during drying and storage

Mold can occur in clean flower during a drying procedure that is not correct, or in storage. The protection is to decrease the quantity of available water:

  • Dry the flower in controlled conditions: cool and moderate, with airflow. Drying decreases the number of yeasts and molds by a large quantity[7].
  • Cure and keep the flower dry. Make the water activity low and stable. A cure at approximately 18 °C (64 °F) and approximately 50–55% RH is a target with a source in the literature[6].
  • Glass is better than plastic. Sealed glass jars give better results than bags: they keep the quantity of microbes low and the cannabinoids stable[7].
09, Health risk

Limits of a visual inspection for mold

Flower can have dangerous fungi when it looks clean, smells clean, and also when a test shows that it is clean. A standard test for mold with a culture cannot always find some Aspergillus that make mycotoxins[5]. The rates of contamination in commercial products are high, and the regulations are not the same[9].

The most important information about safety

The tasks to prevent mold in the grow room are your primary protection for safety. A laboratory test is a second protection. It has limits, and it does not make you sure that the flower is safe. For persons who are at high risk, cannabis with mold is a risk to health[8]. Thus clean genetics (read tissue culture) and a clean room are important from the first day.

11, Important facts

Expected results and limitations

Important facts
  1. Mold must have a set of conditions (humidity + air that does not move + density + moisture). If you remove one condition, you remove the risk.
  2. The mold is in the inner parts of the buds. When you can see it, the spores are in the air. Examine the plants each day in the last part of flowering.
  3. You cannot make flower with rot or mycotoxins safe after the damage occurs. No treatment removes mycotoxins or makes a bud with rot good again[7].
  4. Cannabis can have dangerous contamination when it looks clean, smells clean and a test shows that it is clean[5].

Mold risk is a result of your climate and your airflow. Read the systems paper and the airflow paper. They show how to remove the causes and not only the symptoms.
